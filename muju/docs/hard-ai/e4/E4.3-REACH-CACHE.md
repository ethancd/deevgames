# E4.3 candidate C — the movement-BFS reach cache (`searchFix.reachCache`)

E4 lane 8, 2026-09-18. `docs/hard-ai/e4/E4-PLAN.md`'s "Lane reads and merges"
bullet of 00:35:58Z registers this candidate: E4.1 found that ordinary
positions spend their turn in table construction and named
`bfsFrom`/`bfsMulti` (`src/ai/hard/core/movement.ts`) the two largest
functions inside it, which no E4.3 candidate touched. This lane asks whether
the search recomputes the same distance fields, by how much, and what removing
the duplicates is worth.

The candidate is a PURE OPTIMISATION. It buys time; it does not change one
decision. Its bar is therefore equality, not non-regression: fixed-work output
with the flag ON must equal the flag-absent output on every golden row, and it
does (§4). Every stamp below is from `date -u`; every number carries its

still `4e7afdf76b32fadfab2d11577cb610c8f9f1a491b5153e70b87b0774403600cd`.

## 1. What E4.1 actually says, and what it does not

E4.1's table is a bucket table: the `tables` bucket is 71–76% of profiled wall
time on ordinary positions at every budget, and `bfsFrom`/`bfsMulti` are the
two largest FUNCTIONS inside that bucket. The bucket is not the two functions.
This lane's own measurement of the same instrument, on the same 16 `e1-dev`
turn-6 positions at fixed:100,000: `bfsFrom` is the single largest function in
the run at 1,858.1 ms, and `src/ai/hard/core/movement.ts` sums to 2,282 ms of
20,174 ms of `searchMs` (summed over the 50 largest self-time functions the
artifact keeps per position, so a LOWER bound on the file's true share).
Movement BFS is therefore of order a tenth of an ordinary turn, not three
quarters of it, and a perfect free memo could not have bought more than that.
That is the ceiling this candidate was measured against, and it is stated here
because the lane brief carried E4.1's summary line in its stronger reading.

## 2. The hit-rate census (the measurement that chose the design)

Instrument: a temporary counter inside `bfsFrom`/`bfsMulti` and
`DirectMappedDistanceCache.get`, plus a simulated bounded table at four sizes,
run on the 16 `e1-dev` turn-6 positions at fixed:100,000 through one
`hard@desktop` engine process (`lab/hard-ai/bench/profile.ts
buildE1DevPositions`, the set E4.1 used, seeds 20264100..20264115). The
instrument was reverted before any committed code was written — it exists only
in this measurement, and nothing under `src/ai/hard` carries a counter.

- `DistanceCache.get` was called 27,118,221 times over the 16 searches and the
  existing per-`NodeTables` direct-mapped cache answered 20,858,951 of them,
  a 76.9% first-level hit rate.
- The 6,259,270 calls it did not answer ran `bfsFrom`; 3,774,260 of those
  carried a distinct `(occupancy, origin)` key, so 39.7% of the single-source
  BFS runs in a search recompute a field that same search already computed.
- `bfsMulti` ran 3,506,474 times and carried 1,163,963 distinct
  `(occupancy, sources)` keys — 66.8% duplication, and no first level at all:
  `DistanceCache.multi` has never been cached.
- Over both: 9,765,744 BFS runs, 4,938,223 distinct keys, 49.4% duplication.
  That is the ceiling an UNBOUNDED memo would reach.
- 13,312 of the 3,506,474 multi-source calls (0.38%) come from
  `eval/invariants.ts selfBlock`, which passes a modified occupancy and not the
  state's own. They are left uncached; at 0.38% they do not justify a signature.

A bounded direct-mapped table with full-key verification, simulated on the same
run, against that 49.4% ceiling (hit rate over ALL 9,765,744 BFS runs):

| entries | single-source | multi-source | overall | memory per engine |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2^12 | 21.6% | 53.9% | 33.2% | 1.0 MB |
| 2^14 | 26.0% | 60.4% | 38.3% | 4.2 MB |
| 2^16 | 31.7% | 64.3% | 43.4% | 16.6 MB |
| 2^18 | 36.5% | 66.0% | 47.1% | 66.4 MB |

2^16 was chosen: it buys 5.1 points over 2^14 for 12 MB, and 2^18 buys 3.7
more for 50 MB, which is where the curve stops paying on a box that runs eight
shards.

**Why design (a), the shared bounded memo, and not (b) or (c).** A per-NODE
memo (b) is strictly weaker than what already exists: `NodeTables.dist` is
per-PLY, is never invalidated during a search, and already spans one node's
generation, ordering and evaluation — it is the thing that answers 76.9% of
calls. The duplication the census found is precisely the part a per-node memo
cannot see: the same field asked for at a different ply, by the evaluator's own
tables, or by the `Replica`'s cache. An incremental update (c) is not
output-identical in any cheap form: freeing one occupied square can lower
distances across the whole board, so the update is a re-expansion, and the
callers ask from many different origins rather than repeatedly from one, so
there is no parent field to update. (a) is also the only one of the three whose
identity argument needs no argument at all — see §3.

## 3. The design

One `ReachMemo` per engine, shared by every `DistanceCache` that engine owns,
consulted only where the first level has already missed.

- `src/ai/hard/core/movement.ts:146` — the `ReachMemo` interface;
  `:167` `REACH_MEMO_BITS = 16`; `:170` `MemoTable`, one direct-mapped table
  (`:189` the FNV-1a index over the key words, `:197` `load`, `:219` `store`);
  `:229` `DirectMappedReachMemo`, which holds two of them — five key words for
  the single-source table (four occupancy words and the origin square) and
  eight for the multi-source table (four occupancy words and four source
  words); `:297` `createReachMemo`.
- `src/ai/hard/core/movement.ts:360` — the hook in
  `DirectMappedDistanceCache.get`, on the MISS path only, after the existing
  `occHash`-keyed lookup has failed.
- `src/ai/hard/core/movement.ts:376` — the hook in `DistanceCache.multi`,
  which had no cache of any kind before.
- `src/ai/hard/core/movement.ts:403` — `createDistanceCache(bits, memo)`; the
  memo defaults to `null`, which is what every existing call site gets.

Flag plumbing, one optional parameter per file, `null` at every existing call
site (the minimal path from the flag to the BFS, stated in full):

- `src/ai/hard/config.ts:250` — `SearchFix.reachCache?: boolean`, the optional
  key `makeConfig` never writes.
- `src/ai/hard/engine.ts:233` — `const reachMemo = config.searchFix?.reachCache
  === true ? createReachMemo() : null`, the only place a memo is ever created
  in production, handed to the three owners below.
- `src/ai/hard/tables/context.ts:138` — `allocTables(memo = null)`, `:144` the
  per-ply `NodeTables.dist`.
- `src/ai/hard/core/state.ts:435` — `new Replica(cat, memo = null)`, `:437` the
  replica's own distance cache.
- `src/ai/hard/eval/evaluate.ts:87` — `new Evaluator(rep, w, fix, memo = null)`,
  `:90` the evaluator's own tables.

**Why a hit is identical, by construction and not by luck.** `bfsFrom` and
`bfsMulti` are pure functions of their arguments: they fill the output with
`-1`, seed the origin or the sources, and expand rings over the complement of
the occupancy, with no clock, no randomness and no state outside module
scratch. A probe compares the COMPLETE key word for word, not a hash of it, so
a hit can only return bytes that an earlier run of the same pure function wrote
for the same input. There is no eviction policy to get wrong (a slot is
overwritten) and no invalidation to miss (board geometry is constant, so an
entry never goes stale; `DistanceCache.invalidate` deliberately does not touch
the memo). And because the memo sits BEHIND the existing first-level cache, the
champion's own `occHash`-keyed hit path — including its 1-in-2^32 collision
behaviour — is reached and resolved exactly as it is with the flag absent.

Cost when the flag is absent: no memo is allocated, and the only change on the
champion's path is a `null` field compare on a first-level MISS (23.1% of
`get` calls) and on each `multi` call. Cost when it is on: 16.6 MB per engine,
allocated once at construction.

completes MORE iterations on 1 of 12 positions — `g2-s20_3_15-A-white` goes

12. On the other 11 positions the completed-iteration count is unchanged. On
`e1-dev` at wall:3000 nothing moves: 0 of 16 positions gain an iteration, and 15
of 16 are work-limited rather than clock-limited at this allowance, so a faster
search there finishes the same rung sooner and stops. Work reached at wall:3000
is +0.30% on `e1-dev` (1,158,734 → 1,162,154 units over 16 positions) and
−0.15% on `e1-losses` (2,288,740 → 2,285,329 over 12), i.e. flat: at this box's
rungs the extra speed lands inside a rung that was going to complete anyway,
not in a further rung.

## 7. Preregistration draft — screening row on `e4-val` S3

Offered for the coordinator's decision; this lane launches no ledger row.

- Arm `hard@ablate:search-reach-cache` vs `hard@desktop`, wall:3000, h0/h3,
  `--legality strict`, `--pairs 8`, one commit for both arms, per-game load
  recorded.
- Openings `e4-val.jsonl` rows 32–47 (block S3, released by lane 5's close of
  candidate B). Seed assigned by the coordinator from the ledger rule
  `20260940 + <row ordinal>`.
- Preregistered prediction, in the terms the usefulness rule requires. THE
  COMPLETION CLAIM IS WEAK AND IS STATED AS WEAK: the arm's measured effect is
  2.7–8.8% less wall time per search at equal output, and E2 priced an
  iteration at a median 9.05× the previous one, so a 3–9% saving cannot be
  expected to buy an iteration except where a turn was about to abort within
  that margin. The prediction is: on the row's own turns the arm completes at
  least as many iterations as the champion on every position and strictly more
  on between 2% and 10% of them, with a lower abort share; the measured
  precedent is 1 of 12 `e1-losses` positions at wall:3000 (8.3%) and 0 of 16
  `e1-dev` positions (0%).
- Score prediction: > 0.5 is what retention would require, and this lane does
  NOT predict it. A pure optimisation moves strength only through the extra
  iterations it funds; at 0 of 16 on the development openings the row's own
  openings resemble, the honest point estimate is 0.5 with the interval
  including it. A screening row here is therefore a test of whether the
  completion gain exists at all on validation openings, not a strength claim.
- What would make the row worth funding anyway: the `e1-losses` half of §5 —
  crowded, late-game boards where the saving is 8.8–21.2% rather than 2.7–3.6%.
  If the coordinator wants the candidate priced, the cheaper first step is a
  descriptive completion probe on positions of THAT class (the E1.1 loss set
  and P8's home-race positions) asking how many turns abort within 10% of
  their next rung, since that fraction is exactly the row's upside.
- Tactical non-regression is not required before this row and is not claimed:
  the arm's fixed-work output is byte-identical to the champion's (§4), so
  every fixed-work suite — tactics, home-mate, spawn-strike, exam — returns the
  champion's own score by construction. That is the one advantage a pure
  optimisation has over candidates A and B.
