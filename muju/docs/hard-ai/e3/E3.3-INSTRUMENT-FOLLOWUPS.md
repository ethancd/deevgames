## 1. A9-1 — the invariants EVAL column now reads the engine under test

### The bug

`suites/run.ts` scored the EVAL column with a module-level
`new Evaluator(PAIR_REPLICA)`. `Evaluator`'s constructor defaults to
`DEFAULT_WEIGHTS` and to no `evalFix` block, so `invariantsEval` was the
CHAMPION's opinion under every `--engine`. A7-1 fixed the searched column and
said so; the eval column was left alone on purpose and proposed as A9-1,
because `invariantsEval` is an A6 baseline and appears in M14's and M17's
criteria.

### Before, measured at this commit

50,000 units each, one process at a time.

| `hard@ablate:eval-correct-v1` | 19.6 s | .60 (12/20) | .40 (8/20) | **20 of 20** |

Three different engines, one column, printed three times.

### The fix

`suitePairEvaluator(engine)` builds the pair evaluator from the SAME patch the
searched column's engines come from — `suiteEnginePatch(engine)`, i.e.
`hardEnginePatch` plus E3-PLAN's non-placeholder assertion — and passes its
resolved weights and its `evalFix` block to `new Evaluator(rep, w, fix)`, which
is what `src/ai/hard/engine.ts` does for the engine's own evaluator. The
artifact gains `weightsLabel` and `evalFix`, so a run says what it was read
under.

## 2. A8 / L5-A1 — the recall instrument sees a weights or an evalFix arm

### The bug

`recall/run.ts` built `new Evaluator(this.rep)` — `DEFAULT_WEIGHTS`, no block —
and two bare `allocTables()` whose `NodeTables.evalFix` is `null`, whatever
`--arm` said. The instrument's truth is a depth-2 minimax whose leaves are
scored by that evaluator and whose beams prune by it, so the nine `weights`
arms and the six `evalFix` arms changed nothing at all.

### The control that proves it

`--arm` also flips `--reply-node-gen` to `interior` by default, so a weights
arm is not comparable with a bare default run. The clean control is
`--arm base`, which carries `DESKTOP`'s generators, `DEFAULT_WEIGHTS` and no
block both before and after the fix:

- the PRE-fix `--arm eval-no-safety` artifact equals the POST-fix `--arm base`
  artifact on every measured field (20 root + 10 reply positions of
  `fuzz-1000.jsonl`).

### The fix

`recallEnginePatch(arm)` resolves through `hardEnginePatch`
(`'desktop'` for no `--arm`, `ablate:<name>` otherwise) and throws on a
`version: 0` vector — E3-PLAN's rule, both halves. Its weights and `evalFix` go
to the evaluator and are stamped on `this.tables` and `this.replyTables`, which
is exactly what `HardEngine`'s constructor does for the search's per-ply tables.
`main()` is now behind the entry-point guard `suites/run.ts` carries, so the
module can be imported by a test.

### Before and after

`npm run hard:recall -- --corpus fuzz-1000.jsonl --positions 20 --reply-positions 10 [--arm <name>]`,
20-21 s wall per run, one at a time.

| run | `top1` | `top3` | `replyTop1` | `regret_p90` | `regret_max` | `ceilingTop1` |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| before, default | .30 | .50 | .20 | 1,432 | 1,861 | .40 |
| before, `--arm eval-no-safety` | .30 | .50 | .20 | 1,432 | 1,861 | .40 |
| after, default | .30 | .50 | .20 | 1,432 | 1,861 | .40 |
| after, `--arm base` (control) | .30 | .50 | .20 | 1,432 | 1,861 | .40 |
| after, `--arm eval-no-safety` | **.35** | .50 | **.30** | **931** | **2,505** | **.55** |
| after, `--arm eval-correct-v1` | .30 | **.45** | .20 | **1,222** | **1,951** | **.45** |

- **Default path byte-identity:** the default artifact before and after the
  change differs in ONE line, the `at` timestamp. Re-checked again after the
  entry-point guard landed: still identical.
- On the single fixture position (`recall/fixtures.jsonl`, the F16 punisher
  board, 2.1 s per engine) `regret_p50` is 212 cc for `hard@desktop` and 245 cc
  for the arm, and `ceilingTop1` goes 1 -> 0. That is the test's case.
- The artifact's `ablation` block gains `weights` and `evalFix`; the default
  run has no `ablation` key at all, so the M13 gate artifact keeps its shape.

### The caveat this buys, stated in the module header

For a `weights` or `evalFix` arm the depth-2 TRUTH moves as well, because its
leaves are scored by this evaluator. The cheap list, the reference list and the
truth are then all the arm's own. That is the right question to ask of a
shipped engine — does this engine's beam hold what this engine's deeper look
prefers — but two such arms are no longer ranked against ONE fixed yardstick
the way two generator arms are, and `meanRefCandidates` moves with them
(1,061.2 for base, 1,064.9 for `eval-no-safety`). Read `top1`/`regret` for a
weights arm accordingly; the reference's `GenConfig` is still fixed.

### `rootDiagnostic`

The instrument now sees the arm, so the flag should go back to `true` on the
fifteen arms (nine `weights`, six `evalFix`) — but it lives in `lab/hard-ai/ablate/arms.ts`, which this lane does
not own. Proposed as A13-1 in `amendments/lane13.md`; unchanged here, and
`tests/lab/ablate.test.ts`'s appended block pins that it is still `false` so
nobody reads the flag as evidence of invisibility.

## 4. A9-4 — a seeder DRY RUN, and the ruling it supports

**No decision is taken here, no re-seed was run, and `cases/dev.jsonl` is
byte-identical to the E3 head.** `exam/seed.ts --dry-run` writes NO `dev.jsonl`;
it writes the usual report plus a `reseed` block comparing what a re-seed WOULD
write against the committed file. An unnamed `--report` in dry-run mode lands
beside the committed one under `.dry-run.json`, so that file cannot be clobbered
either.

Measured at this commit, 2,000,000-call proof budget, **40.7 s** wall
(`lab/results/hard-ai-e3/followups/reseed-dryrun.json`):

| | rows | exact | judgment |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| committed `cases/dev.jsonl` | 149 | 127 (126 authored + 1 loss) | 22 (7 authored + 15 loss) |
| a re-seed would write | 141 | 124 | 17 |

The seeder itself carries 123 exact and 2 judgment, and preserves the 16
`source.kind: loss` rows. It would add nothing.

### The four rows the ruling is about

Dropped with reason `dead-position-judgment` — A7-3's proof refuses to carry
them, and `exam/run.ts` already reports them under the `dead` outcome:

- `authored-home-mate-clear-an-adjacent-lane-mate-preference`
- `authored-home-mate-clear-an-adjacent-lane-rotated-black-mate-preference`
- `authored-home-mate-zero-attack-occupier-mate-preference`
- `authored-home-mate-zero-attack-occupier-rotated-black-mate-preference`

### The four rows the ruling is NOT about, and the number A9-4 got wrong

A9-4 says "the dev judgment denominator goes 22 -> 18". It would go **22 -> 17**.
A fifth judgment row disappears, and three exact rows with it, for a reason that
has nothing to do with dead positions: they are hand-added E2.1 cases whose
`source.kind` is `authored`, so the seeder neither produces them nor preserves
them (`keptLossCases` keeps only `source.kind === 'loss'`).

- `e21-purchase-plus-promotion` (judgment)
- `e21-multi-promotion-double-threshold` (exact)
- `e21-summon-and-strike` (exact)
- `e21-upkeep-release-choice` (exact)

That is also the whole of A9-4's second observation, the "126 authored exact in
the file against 123 in the seeder's report": 126 − 123 = the three E2.1 exact
rows above.

### What the ruling therefore has to decide, stated for the owner

1. Whether the four dead judgment rows come out of the file, or stay and are
   reported as `dead` (today's behaviour, no file change).
2. Independently of (1): whether a re-seed may drop the four E2.1 rows, or
   whether the seeder must preserve hand-added authored cases the way it
   preserves loss cases. As the seeder stands, "re-seed" and "drop the four
   dead rows" are not the same operation.
3. If (1) is taken, every denominator in `E3.1-JUDGMENT-CASES.md` and
   `E3.2-PRECONDITIONS.md` needs restating — and against 17 or 18 depending on
   (2), not 18 as A9-4 assumed.
