## 1. What P8 asked for, and what the measurement says it is

P8 §6: "bound the forced rescue injection ... give it a per-generation cap on
`homeWitness` calls (a constant, or a share of the node's meter, charged
through `WorkClass.PROVER` ...), and set `s.truncated` when the cap bites".

Two facts about the code decide the shape of the cap, and neither is in P8.

- **`injectRescue` asks for the witness at most ONCE per generation.**
  `gen/generate.ts inject` runs the eight forced injections once per
  `generate`, and injection 4 is one call to the installed `RescueWitness`
  (`generate.ts:1001`). A per-GENERATION cap of any positive size can therefore
  never bite. What runs away is the number of GENERATIONS that each pay for
  one, so the quantity worth bounding is the count per TURN.
- **Every one of those calls on the P8 position is a total loss.** Instrumented
  at the install site (`search/root.ts installRescueWitness`, a temporary probe,
  reverted) on P8's champion-seat turn 23 at fixed:100,000:

  | quantity | value |
  | --- | ---: |
  | witness calls in one `searchTurn` | 92 |
  | wall inside those calls | 62,820 ms of an 82,254 ms search |
  | mean per call | 683 ms |
  | prover nodes per call | 20,000 = `PROOF_NODES`, every call |
  | calls that hit the node cutoff | 92 of 92 |
  | calls that returned a rescue line | **0 of 92** |

  The prover never proves anything on this position; it spends its whole node
  cap 92 times and hands back an empty line, and nothing bills it.

## 3. The cap, and why it is 8 per turn and not a share of the meter

**Chosen: a constant, 8 witness calls per `searchTurn`** (`arms.ts` sets
`rescueCap: 8`; the key is a number so the coordinator can retune it without a
code change).

- A per-GENERATION cap cannot bite at all (§1), so the budget is per turn. The
  turn is also the unit A14 and the P6 flag measure, so it is the bound that
  means something to a row.
- 8 is what the WORST P8 position actually wants. On the arm-seat turn 25
  32-body race the champion runs exactly 8 witness calls in a fixed:100,000
  search; on the champion-seat turn 23 it wants 92. The cap is therefore at the
  ceiling of observed legitimate use and cuts only the runaway.
- A constant, not a share of the rung: DESIGN F18 wants the rung — and so the
  move — identical on every box, and a budget derived from `meter.limit` would
  make the number of witnesses a function of `chooseWork`, i.e. of the box's
  measured throughput in wall mode. A constant is the same on every box and in
  both modes.
- **The price is not the bound.** Each allowed call is charged
  `WorkClass.PROVER × 1` — DESIGN §8's "PROVER 40 per full-prover call", the
  same price `tactics/prover.ts homeVerdict` pays through its `ProverMeter`.
  That rate under-prices a witness that runs to the `PROOF_NODES` cutoff by
  about four orders of magnitude (40 units against a measured 683 ms), which is
  exactly why the BUDGET and not the price bounds the phase. What the charge
  buys is honesty: the phase is now visible in `stats.byClass[PROVER]`, and
  `byClass[PROVER] − stats.proverCalls` is the number of witness calls a search
  made (the witness does not go through `Replica`, so `countProver` never saw
  it). Every table below uses that identity.

### What the tables say

1. **On the position where the injection IS the cost, the cap removes most of
   it.** Black t23 at fixed:100,000: 92 witness calls become 8, and the search
   goes from 104,977 ms to 44,198 ms on the same box — a 58% cut, and it
   reaches the same depth 2 on the same rung with 6% MORE within-turn
   generation. P8's 62.8 s of witness becomes ~5.5 s.
2. **On the WORST P8 position the cap changes nothing, because the injection
   was never the cost there.** White t25 at fixed:100,000 is identical between
   the two engines in every search quantity — `depth` 3, `nodes` 561, `qnodes`
   461, `turnNodes` 31,753, the same seven-action plan — and identical in wall
   (2,615,726 vs 2,614,986 ms, 0.03%). The champion runs exactly 8 witness
   calls there, so the cap refuses nothing; `work` moves by exactly 320 units,
   which is 8 × `WORK_COST[PROVER]`. The 2,615 s is **4,432 full-prover calls
   through `Replica.make → provesHomeCheckmate`** — P6's ORIGINAL mechanism,
   counted since P6's lane 9 and still not priced — at ~590 ms a call.
   **This corrects P8 §6**: "the cheapest change that removes the 2,641 s tail"
   does not remove it. P8 measured the phase split on black t23 (89% generation,
   60.4 s of it `injectRescue`) and carried that attribution to the 2,641 s
   turn; the attribution does not hold there. The 4,249 s game's tail is the
   `make` prover path, which is a different fix with a different owner.
3. **In wall mode the cap is inert on both positions**, as it must be: P6's
   stop-aware sink already cuts generation at the deadline, so at wall:3000
   black t23 runs one witness and white t25 runs none. The wall residue is what
   P8 §3 said it was — 14.9 s of 26 s is canonical `applyAction` home
   adjudication, outside `src/ai/hard/`, lane 6's P7.
4. So the arm's honest claim is narrow: **it bounds one unmetered phase and it
   is a fixed-work effect.** It is not a wall-time fix and it is not the fix for
   the 4,249 s game.

## 9. What this lane does NOT fix, and who owns the rest

1. **The 4,249 s game.** §4 note 2: its worst turn is 4,432 full-prover calls
   through `Replica.make → provesHomeCheckmate`, counted since P6's lane 9 and
   still unpriced. Capping it is a different change in a different place
   (`search/pvs.ts countProver` would have to become a charge, which moves every
   fixed-work result in the repo — P6's lane 9 declined it for that reason, and
   E2 was named as its owner). It is not attempted here.
2. **The wall-mode overrun.** Two thirds of the 26 s residue at `wall:3000` on
   the 32-body position is canonical `applyAction` home adjudication outside
   `src/ai/hard/` — P7, lane 6's design, its own preregistration.
3. **The witness that never proves anything.** 92 of 92 calls on the black t23
   race spent the full `PROOF_NODES` budget and returned nothing. A cheaper and
   better-targeted rule than a flat cap is available — stop asking once the
   prover has failed at the node cutoff N times in this turn — and it would let
   the budget stay generous where the witness is productive. Filed as a
   follow-on, not built: the cap is what P8 §6 asked for and what this lane
   priced.
4. **Where the cap bites, the whole search stops writing to the TT.**
   `SearchContext.truncated` latches for the rest of the search (the P6 rule),
   so a refusal at one node suppresses every later TT write in that turn. That
   is the shape P6 chose and the brief asked for, and on the measured positions
   the arm still lands on the champion's move; but it is a blunt instrument and
   a per-node truncation flag would be a strictly better one if a lane ever
   builds it.
