# Concepts — three variants

**These are concept sketches, not evidence.** They are 2D vector illustrations of design intent,
generated by `cad/concepts.py` from the canonical glyph artwork and the shared parameters. No
image-generation tool was available in the build environment. The raster prompts for each
variant (overview, construction and tabletop, in the structure of `03-concept-art-prompt.md`) are
saved in `<variant>/prompts.md` for a later run. For the shape of the actual parts, use
`../renders/` (renders of the exported meshes) or the gallery's 3D viewer.

Every board keeps the locked design: both armies in the same octagon, six coloured glyphs with
copper metal, additive T1/T2/T3, loose glyphs for pending summons, gray tiles with ivory A1 and
charcoal J10 homes, and translucent cyan stacks. No accessories appear.

## Comparison

| | Facet | Pebble | Turned Court |
|---|---|---|---|
| Name and language | Crisp octagonal ledges with 45° chamfers. Minimal. | Soft octagon: rounded vertical corners, quarter-round top edges. | Stepped plinth and crown on the base, eight broad flutes per pedestal, raised glyph plateau, court-frame groove on tiles. Informed by the Turned Court / Baroque studies, without their monolithic figurines. |
| Readability | Highest. Tier ledges are sharp and read at arm's length. | High. Steps are softer but the silhouettes still differ by 1.75 mm per side. | High. The flutes make tiers countable by touch. Most visual noise. |
| Tactile | Precise; chamfers give fingernail purchase for separating layers. | Warmest in the hand; nothing catches. | Grippy flutes; architectural. |
| Print complexity | Lowest. | Rounded edges terrace at 0.2 mm layers (finer layers recommended). Largest files. | Moderate. Vertical flutes and steps need no supports. The stepped glyph plateau adds top-surface detail. |
| Construction risks | Chamfered glyph tips are the sharpest points (rounded by the 0.35 mm offset). | Tile top round limited to 0.8 mm (1.0 mm failed in the kernel at the tab necks). | Dust and stringing show more. Do not add finer ornament. |

All three share one interface specification (IF1). Only the external edges and ornament differ,
so the calibration coupons serve every variant.

## Engineering revisions against the concept intent

- The glyphs are the canonical symbols offset outward by 0.35 mm. The **bolt** and **leaf** each
  stand on a small neck over a tang that sits 2.5 mm off the symbol centre, so the whole symbol
  stays visible. See `../research/muju-glyph-reference-sheet.png`.
- Piece widths were reduced from the brief's 28/32/34 mm to 25/28.5/32 mm so a hand-rotated T3
  clears four stacks at 50 mm pitch. See `../research/engineering-decisions.md`.
- The raised glyph plateau of the Turned Court variant is an inset offset. On thin tips it
  simply stops rather than forming fragile detail.
