# E3.1 group ablation — which group's removal costs the most at fixed work

Lane 5 of the E3 campaign (`E3-PLAN.md`, slice row 5), 2026-09-17, branch
`claude/hard-ai-e3-lane5`.

## The answer, in one paragraph

At fixed work (`fixed:100000` per decision, `e1-dev.jsonl` development
openings, 8 pairs = 16 games per arm, h0 and h3) the group whose removal costs
the champion the most is ECONOMY: `hard@ablate:eval-no-economy` scores 0.188
(3/0/13, −255 Elo, 95% interval [−678, −104]) against the champion. SPACE is
second (0.312, 5/0/11, −137 [−309, −14]). HOME (−44) and the twenty INVARIANTS
(0) are inside their intervals, and removing SAFETY WINS at this work
(0.875, 14/0/2, +338 [+168, +1200]) — a result to be read as a question about
the safety block's pricing, not as a candidate. On the examination set's development
stratum (127 exact cases, `fixed:25000`) the group whose removal costs the most
is SPACE: `hard@ablate:eval-no-space` passes 113/127 against the champion's
121/127. Eight of its nine newly-failed cases are `immediate-action`
spawn-strike cases and the ninth is
`authored-tactics-tactics-plugged-fire_2-vs-fire_3`. No other arm loses more
than three exact cases, and two arms (`eval-no-economy`, `eval-stage01`) pass
one MORE case than the champion.

These rows are DESCRIPTIVE at fixed work. They are not equal-time pricing and
no arm is retained or rejected by them:

- Fixed work holds the work rung, not seconds. A zeroed group makes the
  evaluator cheaper per node as well as blinder (see "The confound a fixed-work
  row cannot separate", and the measured size of the effect under "The cost
  side, measured"), so a fixed-work row reads the two together and an
  equal-time row would read them differently.
- The openings are the DEVELOPMENT stratum (`ALLOCATION.md`: `e1-dev.jsonl`
  may be inspected, debugged against and tuned against), which is the stratum
  descriptive rows are allowed to use and the stratum no retention decision may
  use.
- Eight pairs per arm is a small sample. Every Elo figure quoted below carries
  the ladder's own interval, and at this n the interval covers zero for every
  arm unless the table says otherwise.

### Material is never zeroed, for two reasons

- An engine whose `material` params were zero could not see a capture at all.
  Every other feature is a modifier on a material picture; without the picture
  the arm would measure "an engine that cannot count", not "the champion
  without a concept", and its rows would price nothing.
- `w[F.Material]` is INERT in the shipped evaluator, so an `eval-no-material`
  arm that zeroed it would be a byte-identical player to `base` under a
  different config hash — a silent A/A row. `Evaluator.stage0`
  (`src/ai/hard/eval/evaluate.ts:103-125`) scores the material block from
  `weights.material` through `materialCc` and then sums `w[i]·f[i]` from
  `F.Rent` (1) upward, so `w[F.Material] = 100`
  (`src/ai/hard/eval/weights.ts:56`) is read by nothing under `src/ai/hard/**`
  (`grep -rn "F.Material" src/ai/hard/` returns the write, the two comments and
  the feature-extraction line `features.ts:174`, and no other read).

### That the arm reaches the engine

E0's I2 lesson is that a lab engine built from `armHardConfig()` alone plays
with `placeholder-m4`, 58 zero weights. Three checks, all in
`tests/lab/ablate.test.ts`:

- `armHardConfig(arm).weights.version` is `WEIGHTS_VERSION` (1), not 0, so
  `hardEnginePatch` (`lab/hard-ai/bots/hard.ts:286-290`) keeps the arm's vector
  instead of substituting `DEFAULT_WEIGHTS`.
- `hardEnginePatch('ablate:<arm>').weights.label` is the arm's label, and
  `hardEnginePatch('ablate:base').weights.label` is `default-v1` — the control
  that shows the substitution is what makes every non-weights arm play the
  champion's evaluation.
- A LIVE `new HardEngine(hardEnginePatch('ablate:<arm>'))` reports the arm's
  vector at `engine.ctx.eval.currentWeights`: the label, `version` 1, every
  group index zero, and the 18 `material` params equal to `DEFAULT_WEIGHTS`'.

Every ladder row's `manifest.json` records it independently: row 1's
`aResolvedConfig.config.weights.label` is `default-v1-no-economy` with
`w[1] = 0` (`Rent`), and `bResolvedConfig.config.weights.label` is `default-v1`
with `w[1] = -422`.

### The confound a fixed-work row cannot separate

`boundStage2` (`src/ai/hard/eval/features.ts:555-574`) sums the ABSOLUTE stage-2
weights into the lazy-evaluation bound, and `Evaluator.evaluate`
(`evaluate.ts:155-160`) exits at stage 1 whenever `v1 ± bound` falls outside the
window. Zeroing a stage-2 weight therefore shrinks the bound and makes the
stage-1 exit fire more often, which makes the arm CHEAPER per node. At
`eval-stage01` the bound is 0 at every position, so the stage-1 exit fires
whenever the window allows it at all.

So a weights arm at fixed work is both blinder and faster, and its score reads
the sum of the two. That is a reason to read these rows as descriptive and to
price any candidate that comes out of them with an equal-time row, per
`E3-PLAN.md`'s "Strength is judged only by the equal-time row". "The cost side,
measured" below says how large the effect actually is on six development
positions: small for five arms, and the whole story for `eval-stage01`.

### Reading the rows

- ECONOMY is the group whose removal costs the most: −255 Elo, 3/0/13, and the
  95% interval [−678, −104] excludes zero. It is worse at both handicaps
  (0.125 at h0, 0.250 at h3). The cost does not track how much weight was
  removed: economy removes 13 weights summing to 2,737 in absolute value, home
  removes 11 summing to 6,290 and lands at −44, and safety removes 19 summing
  to 1,770 — the smallest sum of the four groups — and WINS.
- SPACE is second: −137 Elo [−309, −14], 5/0/11, worse at both handicaps, and
  the same group that costs 8 exact exam cases. Two independent instruments
  (the exam and the row) agree on this group, which none of the others can say.
- SAFETY goes the other way: removing the 19 safety weights WINS, +338 Elo
  [+168, +1200], 14/0/2, 0.875 at both handicaps. Read it as a question, not as
  a candidate: a group ablation that beats the champion on four development
  positions says the champion's safety block is mispriced or miscounted
  somewhere, not that shipping without safety is an improvement. E3-PLAN's
  rules put any such claim behind an equal-time row on validation openings, and
  none was run.
- INVARIANTS is the null: 0 Elo, 8/0/8, and the 20 invariant penalties are
  worth nothing measurable here. The exam agrees at 121/127, the champion's own
  count. Both readings are at n = 8 pairs and 127 cases; neither is a proof of
  no effect.
- `eval-stage01` (+89 [−73, +307], 10/0/6) covers `eval-no-safety`'s features
  and `eval-no-economy`'s stage-2 features at once and lands between them. The
  interval includes zero.

  the six arms: node counts move by at most +14% (`eval-no-space`) and completed

  explain a 0.875 score by itself, and it cannot explain why `eval-no-economy`

- It IS the whole story for `eval-stage01`'s cost: 570 evals for 567 nodes,
  i.e. about one eval class per node, against the champion's 965 for 537. With
  every stage-2 weight zero, `boundStage2` returns 0 and the stage-1 exit fires

- `eval-no-space` does not spend its budget: 77,238 of 100,000 units on average,
  and 45,767 on `e1-g3-s105`. At fixed work it is therefore not "the champion's
  search minus some knowledge"; on some positions it is a SHORTER search too.
  Its −137 Elo is measured against an opponent that spent the full rung.

## What this lane did not measure

- No equal-time (`wall:3000`) row. Under E3-PLAN's rules strength is judged
  only by an equal-time row, and this lane's rows are fixed-work by design.
- No validation or sealed openings. Development openings only.
- No claim about WHY a group's removal costs what it costs. The exam case lists
  above locate it; the decomposition is lane 4's (`E3.1-CONTRIBUTIONS.md`) and
  the per-feature semantics lane 1's.
- No within-group ablation. A group arm removes 10–19 weights at once, so it
  cannot say which weight in the group carries the cost.
