The question E3 handed over (`../e3/E3-CLOSE.md`, handoff 2, and `../e3/E3-PLAN.md`'s
`ORDER_TT` correction): at `g2-s20_3_15-A-white` white turn 3 the adviser's end
state and the played end state have identical 58-feature vectors
(`../e3/E3.1-JUDGMENT-CASES.md` §1.5, L7-F7) and every root candidate carried the
same searched score, and the played turn led the ordering by exactly 2,000,000 —
the transposition-table move bonus. What resolves that, and is it why no search
up to 800,000 units plays the adviser's turn?

## 1. The mechanism, with file and line

Four lines at this lane's commit decide it. None of them is a tie-break; the
tie-break is what they leave out.

- `src/ai/hard/search/order.ts:92` — `ORDER_TT = 2_000_000`, item 1 of DESIGN
  §5.11.3's ordering list, larger than every other item put together.
- `src/ai/hard/search/order.ts:397` — a candidate whose `endLo` equals the
  probed entry's `bestEndLo` receives it, so the transposition table's move is
  sorted to the head of the root's candidate list.
- `src/ai/hard/search/pvs.ts:687` — `rootIteration`'s `if (score > best)`. The
  comparison is STRICT, so a candidate that merely equals the running best never
  takes `bestIndex`. The root's answer on a tie is the FIRST candidate in the
  ordering that reaches the best score.
- `src/ai/hard/search/pvs.ts:707` — at the end of a completed, untruncated
  iteration the root stores `Bound.EXACT` with `bestEndLo = turns[bestIndex].endLo`.

Put together: the iteration that breaks a tie writes its winner into the table;
the next iteration hands that winner +2,000,000 and searches it first; and if the
next iteration ties, the strict comparison hands the answer straight back. The
root's move at a tie is the verdict of whichever iteration last broke one,
re-served by every iteration after it. It is deterministic, and it is not a
function of the position.

The ordering's own header already says this is a search fact and not a position
fact ("the sort is a stable insertion sort keyed on `(score, original index)`");
what it did not say, and now does, is that item 1's input is the previous
iteration's choice.

**Is the tie why the champion never plays the adviser's turn? Partly, and not in
the way the handoff assumed.** The adviser's turn `987d5cba3b0297a2` is not in
the winning group at the depth-2 rungs at all: it scores 1,002 cc against the
best 1,020 cc, so it is refuted rather than tied. At depth 1 and depth 3 it does
tie, but it is neither first in the ordering (index 2, then index 3) nor lowest
by canonical end key. No tie policy of any shape plays it here. What a tie policy
CAN do is stop the champion playing `c5f8b28ed6e36696` for a reason that is not
about the position.

## 3. The tie policy, as a flag

`HardConfig.searchFix.tieBreak`, added to `src/ai/hard/config.ts` exactly as
`evalFix` was: a new optional `SearchFix` block, `makeConfig` never writes it,
`canonicalJson` drops a key that is not there, every read is against the named
value. It sits on `SearchConfig` rather than on `HardConfig`'s own body for the
reason `iterationGate` does — the readers are search modules and
`SearchContext.cfg` IS a `SearchConfig` — and `HardConfig extends SearchConfig`,
so `HardConfig.searchFix.tieBreak` is the key a profile or an arm writes.

`'end-key'` does two things, at ply 0 only (`search/order.ts:387-388, 428`):

1. item 1 is WITHHELD, so no root candidate is promoted for having been the
   previous iteration's answer;
2. candidates that tie on the ordering score are ordered by ascending canonical
   end key (`Turn.endHi` then `Turn.endLo`, unsigned) instead of by generation
   index.

Plies ≥ 1 are untouched, so the TT move still leads every interior node, which is
where its cutoffs are actually earned. `search/root.ts pickUnsearched` gets the
same treatment for consistency — under the flag a `gainCc` tie in the deadline
salvage goes to the smaller end key rather than the lower index — and that path
is unreachable under fixed work.

**Why this policy and not another.** The three candidates the plan named were a
canonical secondary key, "prefer the shallower-proved", and "prefer the candidate
whose principal variation completes". The second and third both rank a tie by
something the search produced, which is the property that made the champion's
behaviour a latch in the first place; at a position where nothing distinguishes
two end states, the only defensible content of a policy is that the arbitrary
choice be a function of the POSITION. A canonical end-key order is that, it is
one comparison, and it needs no new state.

**What it costs, stated plainly.** Because the decision site is `pvs.ts:687` and
lane 3 does not own that file, the policy had to be expressed as an ORDERING
change, and an ordering change at the root is not free: it moves the first move
searched, so it moves the alpha-beta path and therefore fixed-work scores
everywhere, not only at ties. The cleaner form of the same policy is a strict-to-
non-strict change at `pvs.ts:687` that costs nothing at all; it is written up,
with the numbers, in `amendments/lane3.md` for the coordinator.

**Determinism re-pin.** The arm changes fixed-work output when ON and therefore
needs its own pin, per E4-PLAN's rule for lanes 4 and 5. It does not touch the
champion's: see §5.

At the three rungs the lane was asked about — 100k, 200k, 400k — the policy does
NOT flip the turn.

Why, precisely. At 100,000 and 200,000 units the winner is decided among the 20
candidates tying at 1,020 cc, and the played turn is first among them under both
orderings, so withholding item 1 changes nothing. At 400,000 units withholding
item 1 changes the search order and therefore the depth-3 values (best 2,006 cc
over 19 candidates instead of 1,870 cc over 28), and the played turn is still
first among the new winning group. The policy moves the answer only where item 1
was the whole of the decision: at depth 1, where the ordering's own 580,110
four-way tie is now broken by end key, and at 800,000 units, where the arm
completes a fourth iteration the champion does not reach.

The arm's 800,000-unit row is worth one line on its own: the arm completes depth
4 at that rung where the champion stops at depth 3 — withholding the root's TT
move did not cost this position an iteration, it bought one. One position; no
claim generalises from it.

The proposal in `amendments/lane3.md` would flip all three rungs, to
`041f1fd01bede6ae`, at no search cost. It is not what this lane shipped.
