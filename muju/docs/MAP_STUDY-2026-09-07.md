# Five mineral-map candidates, with v1.3 units fixed

## Recommendation

**Take D into adversarial playtesting first; keep E as its more intricate challenger.** This is a design recommendation from bounded static evidence, not a decision to replace the production map. The shipped game remains A. The user asked for a local comparison of five maps and a static-solver extension, not deployment of a selected map.

Open [the self-contained comparison page](../lab/results/map-study-2026-09-07/index.html). [Numerical results](../lab/results/map-study-2026-09-07/comparison.md), [full raw data](../lab/results/map-study-2026-09-07/comparison.json), and [methods/reproduction](../lab/maps/README.md) accompany it. The page supports all five maps side by side, unit-dependent extraction, opening paths by turn, fresh versus stripped wells, home-to-district travel, stat marginals, income tables, action-reserve probes, financing, unit-role witnesses and local layout perturbations.

## What is controlled

All five maps retain the 10×10 board, original starting positions, five-layer starting wells, 180° rotation, mining-depth semantics, six actions, and the exact v1.3 catalogue. No production engine, UI, unit, or board-initialization file changed.

| Map | Description | Total crystals |
|---|---|---:|
| A | Five layers everywhere | 500 |
| B | Five identical deep 2×2 islands: four corners and center; three elsewhere | 340 |
| C | Deep home squares and larger off-center expansion islands; three elsewhere | 340 |
| D | C plus medium shelves on one route and shallow approaches on the other | 340 |
| E | Deep homes, staggered deep wells, medium shelves and secondary pockets, shallow approaches, modest center | 340 |

B and C share an identical depth histogram: they isolate geography particularly cleanly. D and E change placement and the depth mixture while retaining the 340 total. A versus the others includes a **32% reduction in finite stock**. It is not a pure map-shape comparison.

The rule lists expand inherited rules and are intended to represent increasing descriptive detail. Rule count and neighboring depth changes are proxies, not a claim about measured human cognitive load. E deliberately pursues more of the earlier design desiderata; it does not come with a certificate that all of them are satisfied.

## Findings

### Opening affordability survives, but that is a narrow result

With the original three units, no promotions or production, and a passing opponent, all five maps have the same optimal cumulative income through own turns 1–5:

**11, 20, 29, 38, 47 crystals.**

Every reported value has a legal production-engine witness and matches an optimistic independent-starter upper bound. That certifies this restricted extraction ceiling. It does not prove an opening is safe or competitive, nor that an opponent lets the starting Plant devote most of the shared actions to extraction.

Conditional financed tier timings are also unchanged when supplied those bank-route incomes. This follows from the identical schedule; it is not independent evidence that actual defended tech timing is unchanged. The financing model deliberately omits income feedback and the added body's traffic.

### Deeper investment distinguishes the layouts

The Plant-investment probe promotes as soon as affordable. In the six-economic-action case it reaches T4, spending 17 crystals in upgrade differences.

| Map | T2 cumulative gross | T3 | T5 gross | T5 bank after upgrades |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 23 | 38 | 68 | 51 |
| B | 20 | 33 | 59 | 42 |
| C | 20 | 33 | 59 | 42 |
| D | 23 | 36 | 64 | 47 |
| E | 23 | 38 | 65 | 48 |

D and E's medium shelves pay the first mining upgrade sooner than B/C's three-layer ground. E preserves the baseline's best witnessed gross income through turn 4, although the maximizing routes at successive horizons need not be one common route. D is close at turn 5 with a simpler spatial description.

These are best **found** Plant routes across beam widths 64, 256 and 1024, not proven investment optima. D's width-1024 turn-5 search produced 63 gross while a narrower search found 64; the report retains the better witnessed path instead of pretending width is a monotone guarantee.

Banked cash does not include the value of the surviving upgraded unit. B/C being five crystals below the 47-crystal starter bank is slower cash repayment, **not proof that the upgrade loses five crystals of total strategic value**.

### Reserving economic actions does not erase D/E's advantage over B/C

With only four economic actions available each turn, Plant's turn-5 gross/bank is:

- A: **46 / 29**.
- B and C: **40 / 23**.
- D and E: **44 / 27**.

With five economic actions, D reaches 53 / 36 and E 55 / 38, compared with B 47 / 30 and C 51 / 34. These are legal income witnesses with unused actions, not simulated defenses. Actual protection can require specific pieces, timing and positioning that a flat action allowance misses.

### A concrete caution about the richest regions

At a four-layer threshold, D has six separate rich components, its largest containing **32 crystals**. E has four components and its largest contains **70**. E's shelf and secondary pockets connect to expansion wealth. That connection might support fluid play—or let a force cover too much rich ground together. Connected-component stock is a screening signal, not a measure of defensibility.

This is a reason to prefer testing D first. E's extra elaboration buys only one additional best-found gross crystal by turn 5 in the full-action investment probe and no improvement in the four-economic-action case. Its greater complexity needs to earn its place through actual positional choices.

B's central four cells retain 20 crystals; C/D/E reduce that to 12. B has an attractive, easily learned focal point but a stronger risk of a prescribed central contest. The static study does not establish that such a contest is forced or favors the first player.

### High-tier mining has less total work in every alternative

All B–E maps contain only twenty fifth-layer crystals, versus A's one hundred. **The unique inventory unlocked by Mining 5 falls 80%.** Total crystals falling 32% conceals this much larger specialist change.

Every map still supplies a sole-cheapest single-unit task witness for all 24 types/tiers in the declared grid. That cannot establish that each unit has enough frequent, consequential work in complete games. Most pure combat roles are unchanged because these maps add no movement obstacles or combat modifiers. The meaningful differences in this static screen concentrate on miners and mixed tasks.

This is the main remaining threat to preserving v1.3's tier diversity. It should be tested directly before adopting any B–E layout, while keeping the units fixed as requested.

### Home economy remains substantial

The unupgraded home-only probe earns 45 crystals on A/B/C, 43 on D, and 44 on E by turn 5, versus the unrestricted ceiling of 47. None of these maps demonstrates a severe early need to expand merely to sustain the starting economy. The intended incentive is access to deeper deposits and better position, not an established forced expansion deadline.

### Depletion and travel are distinct speed tests

The regional solver tests fresh wells and a counterfactual with the top three layers removed everywhere. It can start inside the district or include travel from home. In-place six-action extraction often has zero benefit from +1 speed: adjacent productive cells already saturate the local mine/move budget. That does not make speed worthless. The home-to-district mode measures the arrival cost that the in-place abstraction removes.

For example, Sachita traveling from home into E’s far eastern expansion can extract four crystals in six actions; +1 speed raises that to eight, holding the district and other stats fixed. Already standing inside that expansion, the same speed increase adds no six-action income. This is a concrete location-dependent stat value, not a universal speed price.

A fresh shallow well and a depleted deep well are modeled differently. This is essential for judging Plant/Metal upgrades and the eventual geography created by players' extraction.

### Nearby-layout sensitivity is modest but not zero

The study tests **85** adjacent rotational-pair swaps across B–E, preserving total, histogram and home squares. At width 256, Plant's best-found gross turn-5 incomes span:

- B: **55–59**, versus parent 59.
- C: **59**, versus parent 59.
- D: **63–65**, versus parent 64.
- E: **64–66**, versus parent 65.

E's turn-3 result ranges 36–38, versus parent 38. These ranges combine layout sensitivity with a heuristic optimizer's sensitivity. They are not win-rate confidence intervals.

## Validation and next decision

The study verifies **1,370 production-rule replays**, including both rotated seats, all opening horizons/search widths, action-reserve witnesses and perturbation witnesses. The map test suite plus the original static-value tests pass **50 tests**. Type checking passes. Browser checks verify five boards/500 cells, unit yields, square details, turn selection, opening and regional paths, and narrow-screen layout. Screenshots were visually inspected.

Adversarial matches are still needed for central occupation, raiding, spawn denial, defending split deposits, and the viability of advanced miners after the reduced fifth-layer stock. Use paired seats and resource-aware opponents. These tests have **not** been run in this static-only study; the production replays are validation of economic witnesses, not 1,370 competitive games.

The decision supported here is to prefer **D as the next prototype**, retain **E as a challenger**, and preserve **A as the baseline**. No default map has been changed or deployed.
