## 0. Where the bits are evaluated, and for whom

- `invariantBits(p, t, side, sc, ply)` returns a 20-bit mask; bit `i` is SU §7
  invariant `i + 1` violated FOR `side` (`eval/invariants.ts:209`).
- `eval/features.ts:440-443` calls it for BOTH sides at the same node and
  writes `bit(me) - bit(them)` into features 38..57.
- `search/pvs.ts:341` evaluates a leaf from `p.side`'s point of view — the side
  TO MOVE, i.e. the side that has NOT just moved. So an invariant that only the
  just-moved side can violate enters the leaf score as `0 - 1 = -1`, a bonus
  for the side to move. That is the intended direction: the penalty lands on
  the turn that violated it.
- DESIGN §5.13 says the bits are read "on the position after the candidate
  turn". `src/game/turn.ts:19-40` runs `startTurn` for the INCOMING player
  before that position is observable, and `startTurn` calls
  `resetUnitActions(board, incomingPlayer)` (`src/game/board.ts:270-292`;
  packed mirror `core/state.ts:1274-1291`), which clears `hasAttacked`,
  `lastAttackKilled`, `placedThisTurn`, `promotedThisPlacement` and
  `damageTaken` for that player only.
- Consequence (judge 4): at a macro node the flags of the side that just moved
  are intact and the flags of the side to move are wiped. Six invariants read
  those flags — 5 (`F_PLACED`), 7 (`F_PROMOTED`), 8 and 9's chip limb
  (`atkCount`), 17 (`F_PLACED`), 20 (`F_LAST_KILLED`) — so they can fire only
  for the side that just moved. Invariant 14 reads the same flags NEGATED
  (`!killedThisTurn`), so its qualifier is vacuously true for the side to move.
- Measured: over the forty suite positions white (the side that just moved)
  sets bits 1-14, 16, 17, 19, 20; black (the side to move) sets only bits 3, 4,
  6, 10, 11, 13, 14, 19 — never 1, 2, 5, 7, 8, 9, 12, 16, 17 or 20
  (`pairs.json` `firedWhite` / `firedBlack`; sample 40 positions, judge 4 for
  the flag-reset rule). Of black's ten missing bits, six (5, 7, 8, 9, 17, 20)
  are barred by the flag reset; 1, 2, 12 and 16 are not barred and simply do
  not occur for black on this sample.
- Black sets bit 14 on 40 of 40 positions. Black's bank is 2 crystals on all
  forty (`invariants.positions.jsonl`, `players.black.resources`), and the
  "won no material" qualifier cannot bind for it.

## 3. Quiet-clock semantics (question 2)

Canonical facts (judge 4).

- `INACTIVITY_LIMIT = 10`, `INACTIVITY_WARNING = 7` (`src/game/inactivity.ts:3-4`).
- The clock advances at `handOffTurn` and resets only when
  `state.progressThisTurn` is set (`src/game/turn.ts:120-123`).
- `progressThisTurn` is set in exactly one place: `src/ai/simulate.ts:100-102`,
  when an attack removed an enemy unit. Movement, purchases, promotions, chip
  damage, income and upkeep do not reset it.
- The packed replica agrees: `core/state.ts:987-988` sets `setClock(p, 0)` and
  `p.progress = 1` only on a lethal attack; `core/state.ts:1201-1205` is the
  `endTurn` mirror.
- So the canonical inactivity rule counts exactly one thing as progress: a
  kill.

How the engine expresses it.

- `DrawPressure` (`eval/features.ts:292-294`):
  `(lead > 0 ? 1 : lead < 0 ? −1 : 0) × clock²`, weight −8. At clock 9 that is
  −648 cc for whoever `leadCc` calls ahead and +648 cc for the other side.
- `leadCc(p, side)` is `materialCc[side] − materialCc[other] + (bank[side] −
  bank[other]) × 100` (`eval/invariants.ts:374-377`): catalogue material plus
  bank, nothing else.
- DESIGN §5.12.1 row 18 defines the feature as `sign(v0 + v1 so far) × clock²`,
  i.e. the sign of the evaluation's own running score. The code substitutes
  `leadCc`. `eval/invariants.ts:366-372` records the substitution.
- Measured: on 6 of the 40 suite positions `sign(leadCc)` disagrees with the
  sign of the stage-0 + stage-1 weighted sum (DrawPressure itself excluded).
  `inv12-cleave-line-violating` has `leadCc = 0` while the partial score is
  +2,786 cc for white; `inv10-home-reachable-violating` has `leadCc = +800`
  while the partial score is −378 cc (`pairs.json` `drawPressure.rows`;
  sample 40 positions).
- The feature is a STEP in `sign(leadCc)`, so the swing across `leadCc = 0` is
  `2 × 8 × clock²` cc: 1,296 cc at clock 9, 784 cc at clock 7. `Material` is
  100 cc per crystal of catalogue cost, so at clock 9 the discontinuity is
  worth up to 12 crystals of material. This is arithmetic from the weights, not
  a measurement on a position; no position in this slice's sample sits on the
  boundary at a high clock.

Invariant 16.

- `p.drawRuleOn === 1 && p.clock >= 7 && leadCc(p, side) >= 300 && killNow.count === 0`
  (`eval/invariants.ts:351`).
- "Ahead" is the same `leadCc` proxy, thresholded at 300 cc (three crystals).
  It is therefore inconsistent with the evaluator's own score in the same 6/40
  of positions above, and it is inconsistent with `DrawPressure`'s own
  threshold, which is 0 rather than 300.
- "Quietly" is not tested separately and does not need to be: at a post-turn
  node `clock >= 7` already implies the turn that just ended made no kill,
  because a kill would have set the clock to 0 (`core/state.ts:987`).
- The feature pushes the leader toward FORCING progress, not any progress,
  because only a kill changes the clock. There is a second, perverse way to
  reduce the penalty: give away material until `leadCc` changes sign.
- SU §7 row 16 has a second clause — "never force a losing exchange when behind
  and the clock is near 10" — which invariant 16 does not implement at all.
  `DrawPressure`'s sign flip is the only place the behind side's interest in
  quiet plies is expressed.
- `killNow[side].count === 0` at a post-turn node is a full-budget table for
  that side (`tables/context.ts:244-245`), so it does read as "no kill
  available next turn", which is SU's clause.
- Measured on the authored pair: `inv16-clock-discipline-violating` and
  `-correct` differ only in the clock (7 vs 2). The total eval gap is 560 cc,
  of which invariant 16 contributes 200 cc and `DrawPressure` 360 cc
  (−392 cc at clock 7 against −32 cc at clock 2). The clock feature outweighs
  the invariant by 1.8× on the fixture authored for the invariant.

## 4. Healing semantics (question 1)

SU's rule is that real kills, not damage, are progress. Checked every place
damage could be counted.

- `p.damage[...]` is read 29 times under `src/ai/hard`, 15 of them inside
  `core/state.ts`'s make/unmake bookkeeping. The 14 reads outside it are
  `tables/kill.ts:541,715`, `tables/approach.ts:214,327`,
  `gen/purchase.ts:166`, `gen/promote.ts:84,117`,
  `gen/actionsearch.ts:270,812`, `gen/generate.ts:1060`,
  `tactics/prover.ts:489`, `core/zobrist.ts:174`, `verify/perft.ts:184` and
  `eval/invariants.ts:311`. Apart from the hash and the debug dump, every one
  is `need = defense − damage`: damage already ON the board lowers the cost of
  a kill. Nothing scores damage DEALT as progress.
- `kill.ts`'s DP requires the saturated damage axis to reach `need`
  (`runKillDp`, 432-470, and `solveKill` 541-546): a plan that only chips never
  produces a `KillEntry`. `KillEntry.minActions` is therefore the action count
  of a real kill, and `KillAvailable`'s `valueCc / minActions`
  (`eval/features.ts:434`) prices kills only.
- `cat.killsInOne` (`core/catalog.ts:192-202`) is `power >= def`, the one-shot
  plane; it has no damage term at all. `eval/features.ts:248-266` uses it for
  `ElementCoverage` (`elementCoverage`, 253-267), again with no damage.
- `eval/invariants.ts:311` is the only read of `p.damage` anywhere under
  `src/ai/hard/eval`, and it is a PENALTY term (invariant 9). The tables the
  evaluation reads see damage only as a discount on a kill's cost.
- Timing (judge 4): at a macro node the incoming player has been healed
  (`src/game/board.ts:283-289`) and the side that just moved was healed at its
  own turn start and could not be attacked during its own turn. So both sides
  carry `damage = 0` at every macro node EXCEPT an `upkeepPending` node, where
  `startTurn` paused before the heal (`src/game/turn.ts:37-39`). Invariant 9's
  `damagedEnemy` limb and the `need = defense − damage` discount are live only
  at `upkeepPending` nodes.
- Defect found. `eval/invariants.ts:25-28` states that bits 8 and 9 are
  "deliberately DISJOINT", and `tests/ai/hard/invariants.test.ts:161` asserts
  "never both fire". The implementation ORs `damagedEnemy` into bit 9
  independently of bit 8's condition, so a turn that chipped, left the damage
  visible, and still had a kill available sets both. Reproduced: take
  `inv8-no-pre-adjacency-violating` (white, post-turn of turn 6), mark it
  `upkeepPending` and leave `damageTaken = 1` on the black `shadow_1` at (8,8);
  `invariantBits` for white returns bits 8 AND 9
  (`pairs.json` `chipDemo`; the fixture on disk is untouched). Cost: −300 cc
  instead of −150 cc for one chip.
- The constructed node is reachable in a real search, not only on paper: white
  chips a body it cannot kill while another kill is on the table, income runs,
  and black's `startTurn` pauses at an upkeep it cannot afford
  (`src/game/turn.ts:37-39`), so `resetUnitActions` has not yet healed the
  chipped body. The existing test that covers the damaged-enemy limb
  (`tests/ai/hard/invariants.test.ts:191`) builds exactly such a node but with
  no chip of its own, so bit 8 is off there and the contradiction is not seen.

## 5. UNKNOWN verdicts (questions 3 and 4)

Where UNKNOWN exists.

- `HomeVerdict = { RESCUE: 0, MATE: 1, UNKNOWN: 2 }` (`tactics/prover.ts:78`);
  `homeVerdict` returns UNKNOWN on node exhaustion (`prover.ts:752`).
- `Proof = { UNKNOWN, PROVEN, DISPROVEN }` (`tactics/dfpn.ts:30`); the M14 body
  always returns UNKNOWN with zero nodes (`dfpn.ts:74-89`), and `useDfpn`
  defaults false (`config.ts:339`), so it never runs in the champion.
- `ProofValue.UNKNOWN` (`search/tt.ts:257`) is also the value a TT miss returns
  (`tt.ts:277-278`); its only consumer is the `useDfpn` branch
  (`search/pvs.ts:523-537`), which is off.

Where it is consumed.

- The ONLY consumer of `homeVerdict` in the engine is
  `Replica.provesHomeCheckmate` (`core/state.ts:1349`), which tests
  `=== HomeVerdict.MATE`. UNKNOWN is therefore not a win: the occupation is
  left as an ordinary position and the search must keep playing it. That is
  SU §4.4's "unknown (exhaustion) never wins" and DESIGN's "exhaustion never
  wins", and it is the conservative direction for the ATTACKER.
- `homeWitness` is consumed at `search/root.ts:198` to inject the defender's
  rescue line into the generator; a verdict that is not RESCUE yields no line
  (`prover.ts:791-792`), so an UNKNOWN there loses a candidate rather than
  granting safety.
- Nothing in `eval/**` or `tables/**` calls the prover. The evaluation never
  sees a verdict at all.

Answer to question 3. UNKNOWN is scored as the bad case for the side claiming
the win (it is not a mate) and is never scored as safety for the defender
either, because the defender's position is priced by `HomeInvaded` at −4000 cc
plus invariant 10's occupied limb at −400 cc. But `Inv15UnknownAsSafe` itself
is inert: weight 0, bit always 0, no test of its own, no code. The rule is
carried entirely by the prover's convention, not by the feature.

The gap SU #15 actually names is open. SU #15's second half — "never treat a
threat list scoped to existing single hits as complete" — is violated by
invariant 10's own rescue test: `home.rescuers` is a count of existing
corner-adjacent units with positive power against one nominated threat
(`tables/home.ts:206-215`), which is exactly a threat list scoped to existing
single hits, and DESIGN §5.13 row 10 asked for `homeVerdict(bound) ≠ RESCUE`.

Answer to question 4 (invariant 18). The protocol rule IS enforced, in the
canonical simulator rather than in the engine: `finishPlacement`
(`src/ai/simulate.ts:118-120`) auto-advances out of the Place phase whenever
`canActInPlacePhase` goes false, so an explicit `END_PLACE_PHASE` is only ever
legal while a purchase or promotion remains affordable, and `isLegal` requires
`phase === 0`. The packed replica mirrors it at `core/state.ts:1294-1298`
(`finishPlacement`) and `1263-1266` (the post-upkeep auto-advance).
`tables/home.ts placePhaseSurvivesBuy` (261-280) exists precisely to predict
that auto-advance when it emits a `[BUY, END_PLACE, MOVE]` line.
The invariant is a statement about the action alphabet, and nothing in the
generator can emit the illegal word; DESIGN F7's "SU #18 is restated as the
protocol rule" is accurate.

## 6. Home threat and countdown: the same fact priced up to four times

`homeSafety` (`tables/home.ts:198-222`) reduces the whole home question to one
nearest threat and six numbers. Four stage-1 features read those numbers,
`HomeInvaded` computes the `occupied` fact again straight from the board, and
invariant 10 reads three of them:

- `HomeInvaded` = enemy on my corner, w −4000 (`eval/features.ts:168-171,178`).
- `HomeThreat` = `[actionsToCorner ≤ 4]`, w −400 (`features.ts:286`).
- `HomeCountdown` = `max(0, 4 − turnsToCorner)`, w −180 (`features.ts:287`),
  where `turnsToCorner = ceil(actionsToCorner / 4)` (`home.ts:203`). It is
  non-zero whenever `turnsToCorner ≤ 3`, i.e. `actionsToCorner ≤ 12`.
- `HomeRescuers` = `rescuers`, w +90; `HomePlug` = `plug`, w +220.
- `Inv10HomeReachable` = `occupied === 1 || (actionsToCorner ≤ 4 && rescuers === 0
  && plug === 0)`, w −400.

The predicates nest: `Inv10`'s second limb ⊂ `HomeThreat`'s ⊂
`HomeCountdown`'s non-zero set, and `Inv10`'s first limb is `HomeInvaded`'s
predicate verbatim. The invariant is never the superset.

Measured over the forty suite positions (`pairs.json` `homeOverlap`):

- `HomeThreat` is non-zero on 11 positions; `Inv10HomeReachable` is non-zero on
  the same 11, with the same sign and magnitude in all 11. Correlation is exact
  on this sample.
- On those 11 the same fact is priced 400 cc by `HomeThreat` plus 400 cc by
  `Inv10`, plus 180 or 360 cc by `HomeCountdown`: 980 or 1,160 cc in total.
- `HomeCountdown` is non-zero on 8 further positions where `HomeThreat` is 0,
  at ±360 cc each — the graded term does fire on its own.
- `HomeInvaded` is 0 on all forty, so the exactly-duplicated limb is not
  exercised by this suite; the duplication there is a code reading, not a
  measurement.

Reference numbers for the other overlaps, taken on the violating member of each
pair (`pairs.json` `featurePricing`, sample 1 position each):

- inv1: `Inv1SpawnZero` −800 cc, `SpawnZero` −800 cc, `SpawnArea` −120 cc.
- inv2: `Inv2CornerSeal` −300 cc, `CornerSeal` −120 cc.
- inv5: `Inv5PoorMinerSquare` −400 cc, `PstMine` +180 cc, `DepletionWaste` −270 cc.
- inv7: `Inv7PromoteNoRunway` −600 cc, `Insolvency` −600 cc.
- inv12: `Inv12CleaveLine` −40 cc, `CleaveExposure` −240 cc.
- inv13: `Inv13Turtle` −200 cc, `AnchorDepth` −100 cc.
- inv16: `Inv16ClockDiscipline` −200 cc, `DrawPressure` −392 cc.

Lane 1 owns the feature side of these overlaps; this section names which side is
the superset so the two readings can be joined.

## 7. Findings

- **L2-F1** `Inv1SpawnZero` and `SpawnZero` are the same predicate at the same
  weight. `tables/geometry.ts:102` computes `zeroCliff = info.area === 0 &&
  p.bank[side] >= 3`; `eval/invariants.ts:290` computes `geom.area === 0 &&
  p.bank[side] >= 3`. Both weights are −800 (`eval/weights.ts:66,97`), so the
  fact costs −1,600 cc, which is neither §5.12.1's −800 nor §5.13's −800 but
  their sum. Verified by code reading and measured on
  `inv1-spawn-zero-violating` (white, turn 6).
- **L2-F2** Bits 8 and 9 are not disjoint. `eval/invariants.ts:25-28` and
  `tests/ai/hard/invariants.test.ts:161` both assert they never both fire; the
  `damagedEnemy` limb at `invariants.ts:308-316` is ORed independently of bit
  8's condition. Reproduced on a constructed `upkeepPending` variant of
  `inv8-no-pre-adjacency-violating` (white): both bits set, −300 cc for one
  chip (`pairs.json` `chipDemo`).
- **L2-F3** Invariant 10 never consults the prover, and its rescue test is the
  construction SU #15 forbids. DESIGN §5.13 row 10 asks for
  `homeVerdict(bound) ≠ RESCUE`; `eval/invariants.ts:319-321` substitutes
  `rescuers === 0 && plug === 0`, and `rescuers` is a count of existing
  corner-adjacent units with power > 0 against one nominated threat
  (`tables/home.ts:206-215`) — no action budget, no two-hit case, no proof.
  Code reading.
- **L2-F4** Invariant 10's first limb duplicates `HomeInvaded` exactly, and its
  second limb is a subset of `HomeThreat`. `eval/features.ts:168-171` and
  `eval/invariants.ts:319` are the same predicate for the occupied case.
  Measured: on 11 of 40 suite positions `HomeThreat` and `Inv10` are non-zero
  together, identical in sign and magnitude, pricing the same reachability
  400 + 400 cc with `HomeCountdown` adding 180 or 360 cc.
- **L2-F5** DESIGN §5.13 row 3's `retreats > 0` clause is not implemented, and
  the fixture authored for invariant 3 is a case where the clause is false.
  `eval/invariants.ts:281-283` tests only class and cost; `t.retreats` exists
  and is unread. Measured: the sole white RETREAT unit of cost ≥ 4 in the forty
  positions is the `plant_1` on (7,5) of `inv3-retreat-square-violating`
  (white, turn 6) and it has `retreats === 0` (`pairs.json` `approachAudit`).
- **L2-F6** Invariant 4's punishment clause tests the wrong unit.
  `adjacentAttackerKillable` (`eval/invariants.ts:97-110`) asks whether an enemy
  ALREADY adjacent to our unit is killable; SU #4 asks whether the attacker that
  strands itself next turn can be killed. The module documents the substitution
  at lines 94-96. On the forty positions the clause never suppresses a bit
  (`pairs.json` `approachAudit`: one STRAND unit, `adjacentEnemyKillable`
  false).
- **L2-F7** `DrawPressure` and invariant 16 define "ahead" as catalogue material
  plus bank, not as the evaluation's own score. DESIGN §5.12.1 row 18 says
  `sign(v0 + v1 so far)`; `eval/features.ts:293-294` uses `sign(leadCc)` and
  `eval/invariants.ts:351` uses `leadCc >= 300`. Measured: the two signs
  disagree on 6 of 40 suite positions, including `leadCc = 0` against a
  +2,786 cc partial score on `inv12-cleave-line-violating` (white, turn 6).
- **L2-F8** `DrawPressure` is a step function of `sign(leadCc)`, so a side
  slightly ahead can collect `2 × 8 × clock²` cc — 1,296 cc at clock 9 — by
  losing material until the sign flips. `Material` is 100 cc per crystal of
  catalogue cost, so the cliff is worth up to twelve crystals of material.
  Arithmetic from `eval/features.ts:292-294` and `eval/weights.ts:56,75`; not
  measured on a position.
- **L2-F9** Six invariants can only ever fire for the side that just moved, and
  invariant 14's qualifier is vacuous for the side to move.
  `src/game/board.ts:270-292` clears the flags invariants 5, 7, 8, 9's chip
  limb, 17 and 20 read, for the incoming player only. Measured: over forty
  positions black (to move) never sets bits 1, 2, 5, 7, 8, 9, 12, 16, 17 or 20,
  and sets bit 14 on 40 of 40 while its bank is 2 crystals on all forty.
- **L2-F10** Invariant 7's threshold is not SU's. SU #7 fires when the projected
  balance falls below 6 crystals; `eval/invariants.ts:303` fires only when
  `turnsToInsolvency <= 6`, i.e. when the balance goes NEGATIVE inside six of
  the side's own turns (`tables/economy.ts:180-231`). SU's "except for a
  specific kill or survival threshold" exception is absent, and `F.Insolvency`
  already prices the same runway at −150 per turn inside the horizon.
  Code reading.
- **L2-F11** Invariant 14 is a state penalty where SU wrote an action penalty.
  SU #14 forbids SPENDING the last 6-8 crystals; `eval/invariants.ts:343` fires
  on any position with `bank < 6` and no kill, whether or not anything was
  spent, on top of `BankLiquid`'s smooth −90 cc per crystal below 8. Code
  reading, with the 40/40 black incidence above as the measured consequence.
- **L2-F12** The invariants suite is not covered by any vitest file.
  `tests/ai/hard/invariants.test.ts` builds its own minimal boards and reads
  neither the suite nor the positions file (E1.2-EXAM-SET §1 records the same:
  "cites it in prose but reads neither the suite nor its positions"), and
  E1.2 §1.3 could carry none of the twenty rows into the exam set ("paired
  post-turn positions with no root"). The only in-repo consumer is
  `hard:suite --suites invariants`.

## 8. Readings (not findings)

- The evaluation's 0.60 on the pair reading and the six backwards pairs are
  reproduced here through the same `Evaluator.full` entry point the suite
  runner uses, so this is a reproduction rather than a second opinion. Nothing
  in this slice contradicts M14's measurement.
- `Approach.RETREAT` requires only a free neighbour after the hit
  (`tables/approach.ts:344-355`), while `retreats` subtracts the defender's
  strike map (`approach.ts:40-44`). The two quantities answer different
  questions and are not interchangeable; DESIGN §5.13 row 3 names the second.
- Every speed-1 definition in the shipped catalogue has `mine > 0`, so
  invariant 2's `spd === 1` test and DESIGN's "speed-1 miners" agree on this
  catalogue. A catalogue change would separate them.
- Invariant 12 implements a single bit at −40 where DESIGN §5.13 wrote "−40 per
  chain unit"; `eval/weights.ts:45-52` records the decision, and
  `CleaveExposure` carries the graded version.
- Invariant 13's "radius 4" is implemented as `anchorDepth`, a Manhattan depth
  from the corner (`eval/invariants.ts:83-85`), while the same invariant's
  "within Chebyshev 2 of home" is a Chebyshev radius. Two metrics in one rule.
- At a macro node both sides carry `damage = 0` except at an `upkeepPending`
  node, so the kill DP's `need = defense − damage` discount and invariant 9's
  `damagedEnemy` limb are dormant almost everywhere the evaluation runs.
- `homeSafety`'s purchase branch is skipped whenever anything stands on the
  corner (`tables/home.ts:160`), and the existing-unit branch cannot route a
  BFS onto an occupied square, so a plug sets `actionsToCorner = HOME_NEVER`
  and suppresses `HomeThreat`, `HomeCountdown` and invariant 10 together.
