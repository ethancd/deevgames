# Depth economy study — preregistered design

Written before running any study games, 2026-09-09 UTC. Production source is the checked-in Muju implementation; the existing untracked `docs/DESIGN_REVIEW.md` is unrelated and will remain untouched. No production changes or deployment are authorized.

## Hypotheses and decision criteria

1. A (1,1,1,1,1) leaves Plant T3's fifth-layer income too small to repay six crystals plus one additional upkeep per own turn on ordinary routes. B (1,1,1,2,3) may create conditional profitable clusters without improving early T1 income. C (1,2,3,4,5) should shorten payback but also subsidize starting Plant/Water and Metal, potentially more than it improves Plant T3's relative position.
2. Plant T3 should be attractive on secure five-deep routes, remain unattractive on shallow/depleted routes, and lose value under denial or attack. An unconditional upgrade policy dominating retaining T2 across opponent types is a warning, not success.
3. Metal T3's speed and defense may outweigh Plant's unique fifth-layer access even under richer values. Assess same capital cost and rent; avoid crediting all T3 revenue to promotion.
4. Richer bottom layers change reset profitability, but one mining action still resets the quiet clock only once and removes finite physical layers. Check how often late mining prolongs games and whether caps/draws rise.

Recommend a variant for human playtesting only if exact routes demonstrate conditional net upgrade value and full games show no clear loss of aggression/counterplay. Flag >=15 percentage-point growth in investment natural-win rates against multiple aggressive/denial opponents, >=10-point growth in caps, >=50% longer median games, or >=20-point growth in conversion of early asset leads as review triggers, not automatic statistical verdicts. These are screening thresholds, not claims of calibrated balance. No price/upkeep retuning in this comparison.

## Policy diagnostic amendment (after initial scripted results)

Representative A-0-0-0 repeatedly promotes Plant T3, spends all remaining cash, then releases it next upkeep. Existing E13 policies and the copied route-policy extension reserve promotion money but not rent. The initial screen is valid play yet a poor test of sustained investment. Preserve it as `scripted`, and run a separate `sustain` screen with the same 24 pairings, 20 seeds and seats (another 960 games/economy). Wrap all policies symmetrically: queue spending must retain the next on-board upkeep plus the first upkeep of already queued/upcoming T2+ units. The named Plant/Metal investor additionally saves its intended next promotion cost. Promotion can spend that saved money and then mine to fund the next upkeep. No route scoring or game rules change. Do not pool initial and sustain screens. Re-run promotion branches under this reserve wrapper, retaining the same three economic choices. This is a disclosed post-screen policy repair, not preregistered confirmation or game retuning.

An initial reserve-wrapper implementation also required next rent *before* promoting while the underlying queue policy saved only the promotion price. This prevented the intended Plant3 investors from upgrading at all. Its complete legal games are retained under `reserve-v1-diagnostic/` as an implementation diagnostic, excluded from the intended investment comparison; they are not invariant failures. The final wrapper uses the additive savings rule above. No game rules changed to address either policy defect.
