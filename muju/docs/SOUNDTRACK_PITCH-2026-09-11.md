Creative proposal and provider research, 11 September 2026. The initial Hono audition is complete; see [round-one results](SOUNDTRACK_AUDITION_RESULTS-2026-09-11.md).

**Listening revision:** the user rejected Lyria and selected ElevenLabs. Favor the calm warmth of Hono take E and the gradual layering of take C. Reduce exposed chiptune timbres and decorative mallet figures; build momentum through bass, melody and harmonic movement. The [five-song second batch](SOUNDTRACK_ROUND_2_PROMPTS-2026-09-12.json) explores three arrangements per song under this revised direction.

**Next step:** the [Lyria / ElevenLabs audition plan](SOUNDTRACK_AUDITION_PLAN-2026-09-11.md) refines this initial proposal into signup steps, a smaller staged budget, and [nine provider-ready prompt cards](SOUNDTRACK_AUDITION_PROMPTS-2026-09-11.json). Follow that plan for the first experiment; its Starter/PAYG option supersedes the initial suggestion to begin with Creator.

**Working album title: Small Fires, Long Games.** Twelve full compositions: six element themes and six tracks that make an evening of online play feel like a place worth returning to.

The brief: relaxed attention, physical momentum, memorable little tunes, a little wistfulness, and enough space to think. Chip synthesis supplies a shared sound; each element's cultural connection affects musical phrasing, rhythm, or texture.

The current [specification](../SPEC.md) and [unit catalogue](../src/game/units.ts) supply the six elements and eighteen names. Fire/Lightning, Water/Shadow, and Plant/Metal are the three mechanical pairs. The title names tiers one, two, and three across different elements. The soundtrack can make both relationships audible.

## Three directions to audition

| Direction | Sound and emotional promise | Strength / tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Lantern Circuit** | Warm bass, soft house and broken-beat drums, electric-piano chords, brief pulse-wave hooks, culturally informed instrumental phrasing. Usually 104–120 BPM. The room is comfortable; the decisions are sharp. | Recommended backbone. Strong repeated-play potential; arrangement needs personality to avoid becoming anonymous chill beats. |
| **Crystal Garden** | Transparent FM bells, plucked patterns, soft dub echoes, airy pads, a little acoustic friction. Usually 92–112 BPM. Patient, curious, luminous. | Best for lobby, study, and the closing track. Too much of it could lose competitive momentum. |
| **Sixfold Current** | Smooth bass under light breakbeats, tighter chip arpeggios, rhythmic displacement, acoustic phrases transformed into electronic patterns. Usually 124–138 BPM, with spacious half-time sections. | Best alternate for a more energetic online identity. Busy percussion could compete with calculation. |

Recommendation: Lantern Circuit throughout, Crystal Garden's openness at the edges, and brief Sixfold Current passages for Lightning and late-game play. Keep the same bass character, drum kit, and small set of chip sounds across the record.

## Compositional process

1. Establish the listening condition: can someone leave this on for an hour while counting movement and damage? Use a stable pulse, moderate loudness, and restrained high frequencies.
2. Write an original identity before generating arrangements. One candidate is six notes, **D–A–B / A–E–D**, with durations **eighth, eighth, quarter / eighth, eighth, quarter**. That is one bar of 4/4. It rises hopefully, then returns home. This is our proposed game motif, not a traditional melody.
3. Make promotion audible. Tier one exposes the first cell; tier two supplies its answer; tier three introduces a second melody that works against both. Each element transforms the motif to suit its own phrasing. Preserve recognizable rhythm or contour when a pitch needs changing.
4. Give paired elements related compositional behavior. Fire and Lightning anticipate the beat; Water and Shadow answer late or across the barline; Plant and Metal build repeated foundations. These are invented interpretations of the game's mechanics, not claims about the source cultures.
5. Generate short competing treatments of three revealing tracks: Fire, Plant, and Shadow. Compare directions using those same concepts. Keep bass and drum language consistent while changing one important variable at a time.
6. Select a direction by listening, then develop the full twelve. A useful first arrangement shape is a brief intro, two related sections, a sparse passage, and a return with one new detail. Write a continuous game loop and a separately finished album ending.
7. Edit the result. Exact motifs, convincing cultural detail, stable tempo, and a seamless loop require listening and correction. A prompt is a specification, not evidence that the audio follows it.

Suggested audition weighting: concentration 35%, desire to keep listening 25%, distinctive identity 20%, cultural specificity 10%, editability 10%. These are production preferences, not measured results.

## The twelve compositions

### 01 — Muju Hono Tanka / Small Fires, Long Games

**Use:** title and main menu. **Target:** 104 BPM, roughly three minutes.

A tiny plucked figure appears almost alone. Hono's bright answering phrase joins it; Tanka's broad lower voice gives the whole thing weight. The bass arrives early enough that the title screen already feels inviting. A later section briefly lets three contrasting voices cooperate around the same hook.

One soft, intelligible performance of “Muju, Hono, Tanka” can appear near the end of the album version. Confirm the chosen pronunciation before recording it. The menu loop returns to the instrumental section, so the phrase never becomes a repeated announcement.

### 02 — Open Room, Lantern On

**Use:** online lobby and matchmaking. **Target:** 108 BPM, roughly three minutes.

A buoyant bass line, small electric-piano chord changes, and two chip voices trading polite questions. Let one musical answer arrive slightly late, like someone joining a room. The tune works while the player is alone and feels complete when the second voice enters. Instrumental; warm anticipation.

### 03 — Hono / Banked Fire

**Use:** Fire theme and regular match rotation. **Names:** Hi → Hono → Kagari. **Target:** 116 BPM.

Use Japanese koto phrasing as a starting point: precise plucked attacks, a sustained pitch gently bent after the attack, and enough silence for each gesture to register. Koto is documented in Senzoku Gakuen's [instrument library](https://www.senzoku-online.jp/TMDL/e/01-koto.html); the [Italian national instrument museum](https://museostrumentimusicali.cultura.gov.it/strum/koto-cetra-2/) describes left-hand pitch modification.

Our melody is a short rising spark, a bent held note, then a low reply. A dry chip voice echoes only the last two notes. The groove is nimble, with a warm kick and brushed rim sound. Kagari's section expands the register without an orchestral explosion. The emotional image is a hearth that can suddenly flare. Instrumental.

### 04 — Umeme / Sideways Spark

**Use:** Lightning theme. **Names:** Radi → Umeme → Kimubunga. **Target:** 120 BPM.

Look specifically to the Swahili coast: taarab's ornamented melodic world and kidumbak's intimate dance energy. The university-hosted [Zanzibar recording review](https://www2.umbc.edu/eol/7/reed/index.html) distinguishes those related traditions and includes examples.

A short ornamented string phrase passes to a rounded chip lead, then an answering pluck arrives on an offbeat. Small hand-drum gestures and a springy bass keep the movement friendly. The hook seems to reach its destination half a step ahead of the listener. Use one carefully pronounced “umeme” twice in an album-only passage if a vocal experiment earns its place; retain a complete instrumental gameplay mix.

### 05 — Muju / A Field of Small Decisions

**Use:** Plant theme. **Names:** Muju → Sachita → Sachakuna. **Target:** 108 BPM.

Follow the Quechua/Andean side of the game's Plant naming. A gently articulated huayno rhythm gives it forward motion: a longer stressed note followed by two shorter ones. The [Ohio State Andean music guide](https://clas.osu.edu/andean-music-teacher-guide) explains that rhythm and the practice of alternating notes between paired panpipes. [Smithsonian Folkways](https://folkways.si.edu/playlist/a-field-guide-to-peru) also documents the breadth of Quechua-region huayno, including harp and charango traditions.

Our hook starts in one plucked voice; another contributes the missing notes. A harp-like sound and two soft chip timbres slowly form a woven melody. At tier three, a previously incidental bass figure becomes a second tune. It should feel like an economy becoming a garden: busy, generous, quietly unstoppable. Instrumental.

### 06 — Tanka / Weight Without Hurry

**Use:** Metal theme. **Names:** Inyan → Mazask → Tanka. **Target:** 104 BPM.

Develop an original instrumental response to Lakota musical context: a grounded drum pulse and a melody that moves from a high opening toward a repeated low landing. The descending contour is a broader Northern Plains characteristic, documented by the [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/collections/songs-of-america/articles-and-essays/musical-styles/american-indian-and-native-alaskan/); it is not uniquely Lakota. [Sissy Goodhouse's Smithsonian presentation](https://folkways.si.edu/lakota-drumming/american-indian/music/video/smithsonian) provides a specifically Lakota perspective on the drum.

Write a new, restrained melody for a mellow pulse wave and rounded metallic resonances. Widen the harmony as the piece develops, while the bass remains calm. Power comes from arriving securely and leaving room around each note. A Lakota musician's input would sharpen this concept. Keep this demo instrumental; no invented Lakota text, ceremonial quotation, or imitation chant.

### 07 — Straumr / Under the Current

**Use:** Water theme. **Names:** Sjor → Straumr → Aegirinn. **Target:** 112 BPM.

Use a modern Norwegian fiddle connection for the Norse naming: a circling melody around an open drone, with answering notes that seem to keep ringing after the phrase has moved on. Smithsonian's [Norwegian fiddle notes](https://folkways-media.si.edu/docs/folkways/artwork/FW04008.pdf) describe the Hardanger fiddle and its sympathetic strings. This is a later Norwegian folk influence, not a reconstruction of Viking-age music.

Translate that resonance into a bowed texture doubled very lightly by FM synthesis. Set a gently turning, three-note upper pattern over a steady four-beat rhythm. The bass sounds like water passing under a bridge. Aegirinn's section introduces a broad countermelody, suggesting depth rather than aggression. Instrumental.

### 08 — Gölge / One Square Out of Sight

**Use:** Shadow theme. **Names:** Göl → Gölge → Karanlık. **Target:** a moderate 9/8 groove, grouped 2+2+2+3; use a plain 4/4 alternative in the audition.

Follow the Turkish names rather than automatically adding a Slavic chorus. Use a bağlama-like plucked line with a brief ornamental turn, and a nine-unit rhythm whose last group makes the phrase lean forward. Türkiye's [culture ministry](https://isparta.ktb.gov.tr/TR-71007/isparta-turkuleri-hakkinda.html) documents regional nine-unit groupings; its [Çorum music page](https://corum.ktb.gov.tr/TR-58740/muzik-kulturu-halk-muziği.html) discusses bağlama use.

The original motif keeps returning with one note displaced. Filtered echoes answer from the side; the low end stays reassuring. It should feel sly and elegant, with a faint smile. If 9/8 draws too much conscious attention, keep the ornamental phrasing and move the arrangement to 4/4. Do not claim an equal-tempered generator output is an authentic makam performance. Instrumental.

### 09 — Unequal Routes

**Use:** default map/match theme. **Target:** 112 BPM, three to four minutes.

Two melodic lines leave the same starting note and take different routes to a shared cadence. Each phrase works on its own; hearing both reveals the pattern. Warm chords and small crystal-like sounds give the neutral board its own identity. This should be the track that most strongly survives repeated listening. Instrumental.

### 10 — The Last Rich Square

**Use:** late-game or concentrated match rotation. **Target:** 112 BPM, three to four minutes.

As the arrangement progresses, remove a few bright decorative notes while the bass becomes more insistent. The sense of scarce resources comes from having less material to work with. A small harmonic change creates pressure without a tempo jump or rising siren. Keep it equally useful to both players; do not make the music announce an evaluation of who is winning. Instrumental.

### 11 — A Line You Didn't See

**Use:** replay, analysis, practice. **Target:** 96 BPM, roughly three minutes.

The main motif is slowed slightly and moved into an inner voice. An apparent decorative note becomes the beginning of a new phrase: a musical version of spotting another move. Soft electric piano, dry clicks, and a bass line with generous rests. It invites curiosity after a loss. Instrumental.

### 12 — Another Game Before Dawn

**Use:** results, rematch, credits; album closer. **Target:** 104 BPM, roughly three minutes.

Bring back the title melody with a lighter rhythm and a warmer final harmony. Compose separate two- or three-second win/loss/draw cadences from it, then return to the same welcoming results bed. Those stingers are additional assets, not substitutes for full tracks.

One quiet vocal couplet, heard once in the album/credits version:

> Leave a light beside the board.  
> There's another morning still.

The instrumental results loop can keep the evening going. The album ending finally allows the melody to rest.

## Other concepts worth keeping

- **Dawn Repairs Everything:** training music; a bent chord gently settles each time the phrase returns.
- **The Empty Approach:** a sparse, percussion-led alternate for studying movement routes.
- **Three Ways to Be Early:** Fire and Lightning riffs rotate around a patient Metal bass.
- **A Garden of Counters:** paired elemental phrases answer each other in changing order.
- **Borrowed Tempo:** a bass ostinato stays steady while the melody begins at a different point each cycle.
- **The Room Is Still Open:** a late-night lobby alternate with more space and a stronger sense of companionship.

## Voices, loops, and the final production pass

The base plan has lyrics in only tracks 1 and 12; track 4 is an optional third experiment. All six elements retain instrumental gameplay versions. Intelligible singing occupies well under a minute of an approximately 35–40-minute album. A non-English lyric would need language-specific writing and pronunciation review; the generator does not certify either.

Keep chip sounds selectively exposed: one recognizable pulse lead, one FM/plucked countervoice, a rounded bass, and light noise percussion. Continuous arpeggios and bright hats should not occupy every available space.

Produce roughly 90–180 seconds of useful loop material per match cue. Audition each candidate against the actual game for ten minutes and a repeated loop for twenty. Check the join on headphones, intelligibility of game sounds, stable loudness between tracks, and fatigue. Repair loop boundaries on musical barlines, carrying reverb across the join where appropriate. For precise recurring melodies, sequence or record the melody ourselves and layer it over a selected instrumental bed.

Save full mixes and, when usable, separate rhythm, harmony, and melody stems. Stem extraction is not guaranteed to be artifact-free. Prefer one stable cue per match initially; later crossfades can occur at phrase boundaries. Music playback should begin after a user gesture and offer its own volume/mute control.

## Concrete hookup plan

Recommended inexpensive first route: a local generation script using a billing-enabled Gemini API project. Keep its API key in the local environment, outside source control and outside the browser bundle. Requests go to the documented Interactions endpoint using `lyria-3-clip-preview` or `lyria-3.5`. The script saves returned audio plus the request, model, timestamp, cost estimate, and a local review label.

For more arrangement control, the same local workflow can instead call ElevenLabs' official `POST /v1/music` endpoint. Its request accepts either a text prompt or a detailed composition plan. `force_instrumental: true` is available with the prompt route. Use the plan route to place the sparse lyric passages and instrumental sections deliberately. Choose this after auditioning if its results or editing controls justify the roughly $22 monthly plan.

Generate during soundtrack production, then ship selected static audio with the game. Match playback incurs no generation requests. No live music-generation backend is needed for version one.

Use a local request-count budget and an explicit spending ceiling in the script. Provider billing alerts alone should not be treated as a hard stop. The request queue should stop when its approved estimate is reached and require an explicit new budget before continuing.

## Initial prompt cards

These are production instructions, not a claim that a model can reproduce a musical tradition accurately. Audition results against the descriptions and cited listening references.

**Shared instructions:**

> Instrumental background music for a thoughtful online strategy game. Warm, buoyant, focused. Rounded bass, restrained drums, a small memorable melody, and selective retro pulse-wave or FM colors. Leave space for attention. Keep perceived volume and rhythmic momentum steady. Avoid long intros, dramatic drops, trailer impacts, and a final crash. Build a repeatable central section. No vocals, speech, or chants.

**Fire / Lantern Circuit:**

> 116 BPM in 4/4. Intimate koto-like plucks with an occasional gentle pitch bend after the attack. A brief rising phrase, a sustained bent note, then a low reply. A soft pulse-wave voice echoes the final two notes. Warm electric bass and understated broken-beat drums. Quiet confidence, banked heat. Introduce one answering melody in the second half without increasing loudness.

**Plant / Lantern Circuit:**

> 108 BPM. An instrumental melody informed by Andean huayno phrasing, with a light long-short-short rhythmic figure. Rounded harp-like plucks and two soft chip voices alternate notes to complete one tune. Warm bass; relaxed, dancing percussion. The first section is sparse, the next weaves the same phrase into a richer pattern. Patient growth, lively attention, clear space between phrases.

**Shadow / Lantern Circuit:**

> Instrumental, moderate 9/8 grouped 2+2+2+3. Understated Turkish bağlama-like plucked phrasing with brief ornamental turns. A rounded bass and soft percussion make the asymmetry easy to follow. A mellow pulse-wave answer arrives in the last rhythmic group. Sly, poised, inviting, with no horror effects. Keep the same groove throughout. Make a contrasting version in 4/4 for comparison.

**Direction substitutions:** For Crystal Garden, reduce drums, lengthen the spaces between phrases, and emphasize warm echoes and plucked resonance. For Sixfold Current, use a light 128-BPM breakbeat in the 4/4 auditions, retain spacious melodic phrases, and keep the high-frequency percussion restrained. Compose the 9/8 version separately rather than trying to layer incompatible meters by accident.
