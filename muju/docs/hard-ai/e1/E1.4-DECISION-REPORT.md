## 5. Proposed next patch: seed the cold device profile from `calibrate()`

The defect, read from the code (`src/ai/hard/config.ts`, `src/ai/hard/search/time.ts`, `src/ai/hard/engine.ts#searchTurn`):
- `makeConfig` starts every profile at `INITIAL_UNITS_PER_MS = 200`, `samples: 0`. `HardEngine.calibrate()` (DESIGN §4.17, a 40 ms micro-benchmark that returns a measured profile) exists and has no caller outside tests; the lab adapter (`bots/hard.ts`) and the worker (`src/ai/worker/handler.ts`) build a fresh engine per game, so every game starts cold.
- First wall-funded turn: `chooseWork({200, 0}, 3000)` = largest rung ≤ 600,000 = `WORK_LADDER[4]` = 400,000 units. At this box's ~100 units/ms that needs ~4 s; A11's deadline is the 3,000 ms allowance, so the watchdog cuts it: 160 of 200 baseline games (`turnMs[0] ≥ 2990`), first-turn mean 2896 ms. The turn-1 move is then chosen by the clock, not the rung.
- `updateProfile` with `samples === 0` REPLACES the profile with the first sample (~100, measured on a cut search); `chooseWork(100, 3000)` = 200,000, which finishes in ~2 s. Later turns sit on 200,000 (about 1.6–1.8 s) unless `targetMs`'s ×1.5 home-threat / ×1.3 kill multipliers lift the budget past 400,000, where the rung is cut again at 3,000 ms. That is the 200k/400k oscillation and the unused third (mean turn 1951 ms).

The patch (one hypothesis, one mechanism):
- `src/ai/hard/engine.ts#searchTurn`, measured branch, before `chooseWork`: if `this.config.profile.samples === 0`, set `this.config.profile = this.calibrate()`. Plus engine tests for the cold path and the recorded `abortedSearches` / `firstSearchAborted` (A16 fields already in `bots/hard.ts` and `ladder/run.ts`). No change to `config.ts` constants, `WORK_LADDER`, `updateProfile`, `targetMs`, A11 or A16.
- What it changes: the first turn's rung becomes the one the box can finish (at ~100 units/ms: 300,000 → rung 200,000), so the first search completes, its move is rung-chosen, and the first EWMA sample is a completed-search sample. Also the browser's cold start (E5), which today relies on the client's `decisionMs + 2000` watchdog.
- What it does NOT change: the factor-2 rung quantisation, so the unused third on ordinary turns stays; the deadline cuts on sharp turns where `targetMs` > 3000 selects 400,000; the interior beam; anything the loss classes point at. Its expected direct Elo effect is near zero (turn-1 swings ≤ 75 cc in 8/8 analysed losses).
- Hypothesis H_T, falsifiable: (a) `firstSearchAborted` falls from about 80% of games to under 10% and `abortRate` does not rise; (b) `hard@desktop+calib` does not lose to `hard@desktop` at equal time. If (a) fails, `calibrate()` does not predict search throughput and the patch is reverted (E4.3: revert a failed improvement, do not stack). If (a) holds and (b) fails, time allocation was masking nothing and the patch is still reverted as a strength change, kept only if re-proposed as a measurement fix through an amendment.
- The contest: `hard@desktop+calib` (new label, its own config hash) vs `hard@desktop`, `wall:3000`, h0 and h3, 32 pairs (16 per handicap), `e1-val.jsonl` with `--openings-skip 16` (the 16 openings no pricing row has used), `--shards 2`, `--legality strict`, fixed sample, no SPRT. Retain iff (a) holds, W/D/L score > 0.5 with the pair-aware interval reported per stratum, and `overrunRate`, `budgetExhausted`, `maxTurnMs` no worse than the champion's. A 32-pair row prices; §5 campaign 4's preregistered confirmation is what makes a champion.

Weighing against K, resolved by the `k96` pricing row (§4): K=96 scored 0.383, −83 Elo [−165, −9], so K is not retained (E2.3) and the chosen hypothesis is H_T above. The root-coverage question moves to E2.2's instrumentation (expose the root's candidate list with scores and a searched flag in `search/root.ts`; a per-ply generator configuration) before any further breadth arm is priced. Had `k96` priced above 0.5 with clean timing, the chosen hypothesis would have been "a wider root list improves play at equal time" with `DESKTOP_SHAPE.K` 24 → 96 as the patch and H_T queued behind it; it did not. The ordering argument for H_T stands on its own: it is the cheapest time-allocation change, it has no design trade-off (A16 already decided that cut searches feed the profile), and it has to precede any rung-rule change, because a finer or deadline-aware ladder would be steered by a first sample that today comes from a cut search.

Two prerequisites the `k96` row exposed for the H_T contest:
- The A16 abort columns are `n/a` in a Hard-vs-Hard row (`ladder/run.ts` attributes `bots/hard.ts`'s process-wide counters only when exactly one arm is `hard@`). H_T's prediction (a) is unmeasurable in its own contest until the counters are kept per seat (a lab change under `lab/hard-ai/`, no engine change); failing that, (a) is checked on a descriptive 8-pair row `hard@desktop+calib` vs `aiv2-hard`, where attribution works, before the equal-time row is spent.
- Open responsiveness risk for E5's p95 gates (phone ≤ 3,000 ms, desktop ≤ 6,000 ms): a phase the deadline cannot interrupt explodes in some Hard-vs-Hard positions (§3: 20–180 s turns on both seats in 2 of 64 games at 3953411b). A14's 5% void rule does not catch it (2 games), and a p95 gate would pass while the maximum is 179 s. Until P6 explains it, every Hard-vs-Hard row, the H_T contest included, must report `maxTurnMs` per game and hold the row's timing columns as not citable if it recurs.

Alternatives considered, and why not now:
- K change without the pricing row: "a larger K by itself is not success" (E2 exit); the diagnostic half is met, the equal-time half is the row in §4.
- Evaluation work (E3): the histogram's label says "misjudged" but merges discarded with misjudged and is partly by construction; nothing isolates evaluation; E3 needs the E1.2 judgment cases and the root exposure first.
- Reply-node instrumentation (`root.ts` exposure, per-ply generator): needed, and the `reply-wide` null and the 60/213 count are the reasons, but it changes no move and so cannot be the improvement hypothesis; it is E2.2's first item.
- Rung rule (round toward the deadline, or √2 rungs) to spend the unused third: the observation that 7 of 8 first consequential turns finished with 200–2,140 ms unused points here, but it trades against DESIGN §5.11.6's jitter-proof rungs and needs E4.1's completion profile; it is the follow-on to H_T, not a substitute.
