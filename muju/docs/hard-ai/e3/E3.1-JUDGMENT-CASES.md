### 3.4 Two pairs cannot be decided by their own invariant

`build-invariants.ts`'s contract is per-side: "`invariantBits(violating, side)`
must set EXACTLY the fixture's own bit" and "`invariantBits(correct, side)`
must set NO bit at all". Measured with `invariantBits` directly, that contract
holds — 18 of 20 violating members set exactly their own bit for the mover
(inv15 and inv18 set none, as their rationales say), and 20 of 20 correct
members set none for the mover.

The contract says nothing about the OPPONENT's bits, and `extract` writes the
me−them difference (DESIGN §5.12.1). Measured on all 40 members: the opponent
carries bits on **every one of the 20 correct members**, most often invariant
13 (turtle) and 14 (liquidity floor).

Where the opponent carries the pair's OWN invariant on the violating member,
the penalty cancels to exactly 0 cc:

- `inv3-retreat-square-violating`: mover bits `[3]`, opponent bits
  `[3, 6, 10, 11, 14, 19]`; `f[Inv3RetreatSquare] = 1 − 1 = 0`, contribution
  0 cc of a −250 cc weight.
- `inv6-fragile-anchor-violating`: mover `[6]`, opponent `[6, 10, 14, 19]`;
  `f[Inv6FragileAnchor] = 0`, contribution 0 cc of −120 cc.
- `inv14-liquidity-floor-violating`: mover `[14]`, opponent `[13, 14]`; the
  pair still passes, but on the opponent's invariant-14 bit in the CORRECT
  member (`f = −1`, +200 cc), not on the mover's violation.

So inv3 and inv6 are scored entirely by terms the invariant is not about, and
inv14 passes for a reason that is not the one it was authored for. That is an
engine/fixture semantics bug, not a preference: the M12 bit gate
(`invariantFixturesExact === 20`) and the M14/DESIGN §5.13 eval reading are
measuring two different quantities, and only the first is constrained.
