## The answer, in one paragraph

Of the three sub-blocks that partition the safety group, the THREAT STACK is the
only one whose row clears zero: `hard@ablate:eval-no-threat-stack` scores 0.688
(11/0/5, +137 Elo [+14, +309], LOS 98.6%) against the champion at `fixed:100000`
on the four `e1-dev` openings, where `eval-no-anchor` scores 0.562 (9/0/7, +44
[−36, +129]) and `eval-no-safety-inv` 0.625 (10/0/6, +89 [−74, +307]). None of
the three reproduces lane 5's `eval-no-safety` row (0.875, 14/0/2, +338 [+168,
+1200]), and all three are at or above 0.5, so the +338 is not one sub-block's:
removing all nineteen weights beats removing any part of them. On the exam's
development stratum (127 exact cases at `fixed:25000`) the same ordering appears
with different signs: the threat-stack arm passes 123/127 against the champion's
121, the anchor arm 121 (the champion's failure set exactly) and the
safety-invariant arm 120. Everything here is DESCRIPTIVE at fixed work on four
development openings; nothing is retained, rejected or priced.

## What the decomposition predicted before the rows ran

Lane 4's instrument already measured every one of the 19 weights on three
corpora (`lab/results/hard-ai-e3/eval-audit/<corpus>/summary.json`,
`E3.1-CONTRIBUTIONS.md`). Re-read by sub-block, it is a prediction the rows can
then agree or disagree with. `share` is lane 4's `shareOfAbsTotal`: the feature's
mean |w·f| as a fraction of the mean Σ|w·f| over all 58 features. Summing it
over a sub-block is a statement about how much of the evaluator's output the
block moves, NOT the group-sum share of lane 4's group table (that one nets a
group's terms against each other first, and reads 19.9% for all of safety on
`exam-dev`).

| sub-block | exam-dev (n = 149) | fuzz-1000 (n = 1000) | tactics (n = 79) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| threat stack (8) | 16.98% | 13.29% | 19.14% |
| anchor (3) | 2.25% | 4.16% | 2.70% |
| safety invariants (8) | 2.41% | 3.02% | 1.94% |

- `Hanging` alone is 5.06% of `exam-dev` and 5.92% of `tactics` — the fourth
  largest feature in the evaluator on both, and larger than the anchor block and
  the safety-invariant block put together on either corpus.
- Two of the eight safety invariants never fire at all on `exam-dev` and
  `tactics`: `Inv8NoPreAdjacency` (45) and `Inv17SelfBlock` (54), 0.0% on both
  (`Inv19SoftMinerExposed` and `Inv20StrandNoRetreat` join them at 0.0% on
  `tactics`). On `fuzz-1000` they fire at 10.5% and 1.7%. An arm that zeroes a
  feature which never fires on the positions a row visits changes nothing there.
- The prediction, therefore: if the +338 is a judgment effect at all, the threat
  stack should carry most of it, and the anchor and safety-invariant arms should
  sit near the champion. The rows below are the test of that, at n = 8 pairs on
  4 development openings, which is a weak test.
- The rival explanation lane 5 already measured is cost, not judgment:
  `boundStage2` (`features.ts:555-574`) sums the ABSOLUTE stage-2 weights into

  stage 2. `eval-no-safety` measured +18.2 nodes and +0.17 completed ply against
  the champion at this budget — small, but the sub-arms remove less weight each,
  so their cost effect is smaller still and the rows below do not separate it.

## Which sub-block moves the score

No single sub-block reproduces `eval-no-safety`'s +338, and the one the
decomposition pointed at is the only one whose interval clears zero. The threat
stack — the eight cc-scale terms that price a threatened own body — scores 0.688
(11/0/5, +137 [+14, +309], LOS 98.6%) with 230 of the block's 1,770 Σ|w|; the
three anchor weights score 0.562 (9/0/7, +44 [−36, +129]) and the eight
remaining safety invariants 0.625 (10/0/6, +89 [−74, +307]), both intervals
covering zero. That ordering is the one lane 4's contribution audit predicted
before the rows ran (threat stack 16.98% of the mean Σ|w·f| on `exam-dev`
against 2.25% and 2.41%), and it is the block the E3.2 concept names. But all
three sub-arms score at or above 0.5, removing all nineteen beats every part
(0.875 against 0.688, 0.562 and 0.625), and Elo is not additive, so the reading
these rows support is that the safety block is jointly mispriced at fixed work on
these four positions rather than that one sub-block carries the +338 and the
other two are innocent. The sharpest caveat is the sample: these are DESCRIPTIVE
fixed-work rows on FOUR development openings (`e1-g3-s105`, `e1-g2-s40`,
`e1-g4-s250`, `e1-g4-s750`), and A15 makes the openings the independence source,
so 8 pairs of 16 games is four independent positions wide however the interval
is spelled — the per-handicap splits disagree with each other (the threat stack
is 0.625/0.750 across h0/h3, the anchor pair 0.625/0.500, the invariants
0.500/0.750) in a way that a four-position sample cannot resolve. Nothing here
retains or rejects an arm: strength is judged only by the equal-time row, which
for the safety block is E3 row #1 on validation openings, and these three rows
enter no ledger.
