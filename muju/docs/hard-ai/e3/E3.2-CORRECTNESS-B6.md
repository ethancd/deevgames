## 1. The defect

Judge 4 (a canonical fact about the position) and judge 3 (the module's own
written semantics) decide everything in this section. No adviser appears.

- `tables/approach.ts classifyFrom` picks the attacker's ATTACK SQUARE by
  scanning the target's neighbours in ascending square index.
  - The candidate set is `ADJ[targetSq]` minus the occupancy, plus the
    attacker's own square when it is already adjacent.
  - Each candidate is costed `ceil(dist/speed)` and ranked by
    `cost < bestCost || (cost === bestCost && cls > bestCls)`.
  - A candidate that ties on BOTH cost and class therefore never displaces the
    one already held: the first square the scan meets wins.
- The board's own symmetry maps `s ↦ 99 − s`, which reverses that scan order, so
  the two spellings of one position can settle on two different attack squares.
- `cls` and `d` are equal by construction in such a tie, so the class table
  cannot move; `retreats` can.
  - `retreats` is `|reach(attackSquare, speed, 1) \ strike[defender]|` — a
    quantity of the SQUARE, and two squares have two reach sets.
- The champion never saw this: `t.retreats[slot]` has exactly one reader in the
  evaluator, `eval/invariants.ts:294`, and that reader is itself gated on B4.
  B6 surfaces a latent asymmetry rather than creating one.

The five positions, from `scan.json` `rot180[arm = b4]` (sample: 1,000 fuzz
positions, each against its own rot180 + seat-swap mirror, static evaluation):

| id | `Inv3RetreatSquare` position / mirror | score Δ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `fuzz-5150-4-132` | 0 / 1 | −250 cc |
| `fuzz-5150-1525-94` | 0 / 1 | −250 cc |
| `fuzz-5150-1103-127` | −1 / 0 | −250 cc |
| `fuzz-5150-263-483` | 1 / 0 | +250 cc |
| `fuzz-5150-263-488` | 1 / 0 | +250 cc |

They are exactly the five ids lane 12 named, and exactly the five that survive
with all of B1–B5 on (`scan.json` `rot180[arm = b1-b5]`: 5 violations, all
`Inv3RetreatSquare`).

The mechanism, confirmed on all five (`scan.ts` `tieScan`, and the per-slot
trace in `tests/ai/hard/approach-tie.test.ts`):

- The disagreement is always ONE own unit whose `approach` is RETREAT and whose
  attacker's `retreats` differs between the two spellings.
- The slot numbering is mirror-stable in all five (`fuzz-5150-4-132`: the victim
  is slot 1 at square 1 in the position and at square 98 in the mirror; the one
  lethal attacker is slot 3 at square 43 and at square 56), so
  `approachTable`'s attacker ranking — fewest actions, then class, then existing
  before purchase, then lowest slot — is NOT what differs.
- On `fuzz-5150-4-132` `classifyApproach` returns `cls = RETREAT` and `d = 2`
  from both 43 and 56, and `retreats` 0 in the position and 1 in the mirror.
  That is the whole defect: same attacker, same class, same cost, two attack
  squares.
- The other four resolve the same way, in both directions:
  `fuzz-5150-1525-94` slot 5 reads 0 in the position and 2 in the mirror;
  `fuzz-5150-263-483` and `-488` slot 9 read 2 in the position and 0 in the
  mirror; `fuzz-5150-1103-127` moves in the MIRROR spelling only (slot 1 at
  square 45 reads 0 with the flag off and 4 with it on), which is why the
  position-side tie scan below counts four moves and the mirror scan five.

## 3. The fix, and what it measures

- The tie is broken in the ATTACKER's own corner-relative frame — key `q` for
  White, whose corner is square 0, and `99 − q` for Black, whose corner is
  square 99 — the same total order `tables/economy.ts bestRelocationTarget`
  uses under B2 (`rot180TieOrder`).
- The mirror transform is rot180 AND seat swap, so it carries that frame with
  it: an attack square at `q` for a White attacker becomes `99 − q` for a Black
  attacker, and the key is the same number. The two spellings therefore choose
  mirrored squares.
- rot180 + seat-swap mirror scan, `fuzz-1000`, 1,000 positions, static
  evaluation of all 58 features (`scan.json` `rot180`):

| arm | mirror violations | of them `Inv3RetreatSquare` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| flags off (the champion) | 585 | 0 |
| B4 alone | 587 | 5 |
| **B6 alone** | **585** | **0** |
| B4 + B6 | 585 | 0 |
| B1–B5 (`amendments/lane12.md` A2's "all five") | 5 | 5 |
| **B1–B6** | **0** | **0** |

  - The 585 that B6 does not touch are B2's economy tie (`RelocationDebt` 501,
    `EconDelta` 342, `DepletionWaste` 318 positions), reproduced here to the
    position from `E3.2-CORRECTNESS-ARM.md` §2.
  - B4 alone is 587, not 585 + 5, because two of the five also carry an economy
    disagreement.
  - B6 alone equals the champion's 585 with 0 `Inv3` violations, because B4 is
    off and nothing reads `retreats`.
- Tie-free invariance, the same corpus (`scan.json` `tieScan`): where no tie
  exists, `retreats` may not move.
  - "Tie" is taken from the code: for a defender slot, every enemy attacker —
    existing units in both forms, and every affordable tier-1 purchase from the
    spawn mask — is costed over `classifyFrom`'s own candidate set, and the slot
    is TIE-FREE when no attacker has two candidates at the cheapest cost. The
    class filter and the action budget are deliberately not applied: both only
    shrink the candidate set, so a slot called tie-free is tie-free under the
    real predicate too.
  - 7,137 (position, defender slot) pairs have an approach at all; 5,390 of them
    are RETREAT; 650 are tie-free.
  - `retreats` moves at 4 slots with the flag on, and at 0 of the 650 tie-free
    slots.
  - `approach` moves at 0 of the 7,137 (asserted inside the scan, which throws
    if a class ever changes).

## 7. What this does not settle

- Strength. Nothing here is a contest row. B6 alone cannot have an Elo: with B4
  off its play is byte-identical to the champion's, and the only row it could
  ever move is one that also carries B4.
- Whether B6 belongs in `eval-correct-v1`. The bundle carries B4, so it carries
  the residue; adding B6 removes it. That is the coordinator's call after the
  per-flag rows, and this lane did not take it.
- Whether the corner-relative frame is the RIGHT spelling. It is A
  rotation-invariant order, chosen because B2 already uses it and the mirror
  test is written in it. Any tie-break that commutes with rot180 + seat swap
  would score identically on a mirror pair and could differ from this one on the
  underlying position — the same caveat `E3.2-CORRECTNESS-ARM.md` §6 records for
  B2.
- The OTHER scan-order tie in the same file. `approachTable considerCandidate`
  ranks attackers by (fewest actions, class, existing before purchase, lowest
  slot / tier-1 index), and a slot number is not a mirror-invariant quantity in
  general. It is not implicated in any of the five ids (the slots are
  mirror-stable there) and there is no residual violation left on `fuzz-1000`
  with B1–B6 on (0 of 1,000), so it is unmeasured rather than acquitted.
  Recorded as `amendments/lane14.md` A4.
- An authored fixture pair for the tie, of the kind B4 has. The five corpus
  positions are real and reproduce exactly, and a hand-built tie needs a boxed-in
  defender (a mobile defender's strike map covers everything within four steps,
  so `retreats` is 0 from either square and the tie has nothing to disagree
  about); it was not attempted rather than faked.
- Cost. B6 adds one comparison per tied candidate and nothing else; the two exam
  runs are 18.8 s each, which is a tie, and no cost probe was run.
