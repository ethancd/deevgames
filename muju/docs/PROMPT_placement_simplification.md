## The rules

Replace §2 (turn structure), §5.2 (build queue), §5.3 (placement), §5.4 (promotion), and §8 (hidden information) with the following.

**A turn has two phases.**

1. **Place phase.** In any order:
   - **Promote** any unit that was on the board at the start of this phase to the next tier of its element, paying the cost difference. Each unit at most once per turn. Tier 3 is terminal. **A unit placed this turn cannot be promoted this turn.**
   - **Buy** any number of **tier-1** units, paying each unit's cost, and place each on an empty square inside an unblocked spawn rectangle (existing rule: corner to any friendly anchor, void if any enemy is inside). There is no limit other than crystals and empty squares.
   - Placed and promoted units **act immediately** (no summoning sickness — unchanged from v1.1).
2. **Action phase.** Six shared actions, as now.

There is no queue phase. The turn ends when the action phase ends; the existing end-of-turn steps (passive income if on the mining base; inactivity counter; draw check) run as they do at your base.

Turn-start order, unchanged except for the deleted queue step: home occupation / elimination → inactivity draw (if your base checks it there) → upkeep → heal and flag reset → place phase.

**Deleted outright:** the build queue and everything that referenced it (queue phase, `QUEUE_UNIT`, `buildQueue`, `turnsRemaining`, readiness, queue persistence, the tech re-check at placement, "queued units don't prevent loss"); **build times** (remove the column from the catalogue and the spec tables); the **tech requirement** (it is now structural — every tier-2 was a tier-1 on the board, so "same element at tier N−1 on the board" is a description, not a rule; say this in the spec); **hidden information** (the stockpile is public; `resourcesSpent` / `resourcesManifested` collapse to one public `resources` plus `resourcesGained`; the opponent's bank is displayed as a number, not "Hidden"); the AI's **observation layer, belief model, particle filter, and re-determinization** (the game is perfect-information; the MCTS becomes plain MCTS over the real state).

**Unchanged:** the catalogue's stats and costs (tier-1 costs 1/1/2/2/3/3; promotion costs are the existing differences; tier 3 is the cap); Cleave; elements; six actions; dawn heal; spawn rectangles and infiltration blocking; home occupation (an invader on your corner still voids every rectangle, so you cannot buy while invaded); upkeep (T1 free, T1 retained, keep-set choice; a unit promoted this turn owes rent from your next turn); the inactivity draw; passive mining and the 4/8/10 map if present.

Design rationale, for the spec, J-016, and the report: the queue's four jobs were hidden information, build-time delay, persistence through blocked spawns, and the three-phase turn. Hidden information produced surprises rather than inference (elegance comparison; design review §6). Build time did no work for tier-1s — crystals earned at the end of turn N−1 became a Hi acting at N either way — and the tier-2/3 delay is reproduced by the once-per-turn promotion climb (Hono one turn after the Hi, Kagari two), which is also now visible and contestable. Persistence is replaced by the bank. Removing the queue makes the tech requirement structural, the stockpile public, and the belief layer unnecessary. **No promotion on the placement turn is deliberate and load-bearing**: it keeps the climb to tier 3 at two full opponent turns of exposure, and it keeps mass tier-1 from converting to tier-2 bodies in the same turn it is bought. Haste for placed units is retained because it is the existing rule, all September evidence was measured under it, and its two sharp lines (the Lightning-I corner sprint, the summon-and-strike) already exist hidden and have answers (Guard; rectangle blocking, the six-action cost, Cleave).

Record **J-016** in the house format with the alternatives considered and rejected: end-of-turn placement (skips the anchor-survival test — a fragile forward anchor could summon behind itself before the opponent can kill it); start-of-turn placement with summoning sickness (a nerf to the existing rule, not needed); build-only with no promotion (loses grow-in-place, punishes speed-1 lines, keeps the tech rule); promotion on the placement turn (collapses the climb; rejected by the designer).
