## Conventions the table below uses

- A feature's value is the symmetric difference `f(me) − f(them)`
  (`features.ts:4-19`), and the score is `Σ_i w[i]·f[i]` in integer
  centi-crystals (`evaluate.ts:114-120`).
- `div100(x) = (x / 100) | 0` (`features.ts:143-146`) turns a centi-crystal
  quantity into CRYSTALS, and is applied to the already-formed difference, so
  the truncation is exactly antisymmetric.
- "unit" below is the unit of the number the code writes into `out[i]`.
  "weight unit" is what DESIGN §5.12.1's `w` column reads as ("cc per unit of
  feature"), which is only meaningful if the two agree; §(b) lists where they
  do not.
- Sign: every feature is `me − them`, penalties included, so a penalty the
  other side commits is a plus for me.

## (a) Double counting

Each pair states what the predicates are (identical / nested / overlapping /
disjoint) from the code, with the corpus measurement that pins it.

### Identical predicates

- **SpawnZero (9, −800) vs Inv1SpawnZero (38, −800)** — IDENTICAL.
  `geometry.ts:102` sets `zeroCliff = area === 0 && bank >= 3`;
  `invariants.ts:290` tests `geom.area === 0 && p.bank[side] >= 3`.
  The two feature values were equal in 1,000/1,000 positions. Effective weight
  **−1,600 cc**, not the −800 DESIGN states in either row.
- **RunwayCliff (25, −600) vs Insolvency (26, −150)** — RunwayCliff is
  `turnsToInsolvency == 1`, the first rung of Insolvency's ramp.
  `economy.ts:177` seeds `running = p.bank[side]` and `economy.ts:222-226`
  sets `turnsToInsolvency = 1` exactly when `bank + income[0] − upkeep[0] < 0`,
  which is `features.ts:417-418`'s predicate character for character. A turn-1
  cliff therefore costs **−600 + −750 = −1,350 cc**.
- **Rent (1) vs EconDelta (23)** — the SAME upkeep sum, twice.
  `rentCrystals` (`features.ts:158-166`) is `Σ cat.upkeep` over live units;
  `economy.ts:175` computes `upkeepPerTurn = upkeepDue(p, side)`, the same sum
  (`income.ts:94-102`), and subtracts it inside `stream` on all six horizon
  turns (`economy.ts:221`). DESIGN §5.12's headline — "Rent is charged **once**,
  as `Rent`" — does not hold: subtracting `pstSum` inside EconDelta removes the
  INCOME double count only, because `PST_MINE` is rent-free by construction
  (`income.ts:16-19`). Measured discount factor of the upkeep leg: **381.7 cc
  per crystal of upkeep** (mean over the 655 side-positions with upkeep > 0;
  analytic `Σ_{t=2..7} 0.9^t = 3.7953`). At `w[EconDelta] = 80` that is
  −305 cc, on top of `Rent`'s −422: **−727 cc per crystal of upkeep per turn,
  1.72 × `RENT_PV`**.
- **HomeInvaded (4, −4000) vs Inv10HomeReachable (47, −400)** — nested and
  always co-firing. `homeInvaded` (`features.ts:168-171`) and `home.occupied`
  (`home.ts:219`) are the same predicate, and `invariants.ts:319` sets bit 10
  on `home.occupied === 1` alone. Measured: 42/2,000 side-positions have
  `occupied === 1`, and bit 10 was set in **42/42**. An enemy on my corner
  costs −4,400 cc.

### Nested predicates

- **CornerSeal (12, −60 per neighbour) vs Inv2CornerSeal (39, −300)** —
  Inv2 is the `cornerNeighboursHeld === 2` case of CornerSeal's 0..2 count
  (`geometry.ts:139-147`, `invariants.ts:293`). Sealing both neighbours costs
  −120 − 300 = **−420 cc**. Measured: `held == 2` in 8/2,000 side-positions,
  `held > 0` in 361.
- **HomeThreat (13, −400) vs Inv10 (47, −400)** — Inv10's second clause is

  therefore costs −400 (HomeThreat) −400 (Inv10) plus up to −540

  numbers all come from the single `nearestThreat` computation

- **BankLiquid (2, 90/crystal) vs Inv14LiquidityFloor (51, −200)** — Inv14 is
  a step at `bank < 6` on the same quantity, gated by "won no material"
  (`invariants.ts:343`). Measured: `bank < 6` in 1,873/2,000 side-positions and
  bit 14 set in 1,742 — 93% of the time the gate is inert, so Inv14 is in
  practice a second bank term. Marginal value of a banked crystal below 6, all
  three bank features together: 90 (BankLiquid) + 20 (BankConvertible) = 110 cc,
  with a −200 step at the 6th.
- **DrawPressure (18) vs Inv16ClockDiscipline (53)** — both read the same
  `leadCc` and the same clock; Inv16 additionally needs `killNow[me]` empty.
  Not separable on this corpus: Inv16 fired 0/2,000, DrawPressure was non-zero
  in 42/1,000.

## (b) Scale mismatches

- **EconDelta over-subtracts the PstMine baseline.** `PstMine` adds
  `60 × div100(pstΔ)` and `EconDelta` subtracts `80 × div100(pstΔ)` inside the
  same score (`features.ts:278,415`). Holding the six-turn `stream` fixed, the
  net weight on a side's standing 12-turn mining PV is **60 − 80 = −20 cc per
  crystal**, i.e. negative. DESIGN §5.12.1's rationale for row 23 is "no double
  count", and the subtraction is the right shape; the two weights are not the
  same number, so the baseline is removed at 133% of the rate it was added.
  For a healthy miner the combined coefficient is about 0.25 of its computed
  PST PV, not the 0.6 row 5's weight announces.
- **DepletionWaste re-counts the economy DP undiscounted.**
  `waste += mine − take` runs in the same loop that builds `stream`
  (`economy.ts:204,221`), so, excluding relocation travel turns,
  `waste ≡ 6·Σmine − Σ_t income_t` — measured EXACT in **1,659/2,000**
  side-positions, mean deviation 2.92 crystals in the remaining 341. The
  feature is therefore `+30 cc per crystal of realised six-turn income` minus
  `180 cc per point of mine rate`, laid on top of EconDelta's discounted
  version of the same income. Mean |contribution| 277 cc; 1,650 cc in one exam
  loss root (`loss-g4-s10_0_8-B-white-t7`, black, turn 7).
- **RelocationDebt is a price divided into crystals and re-priced at 0.6.**
  `economy.ts:210` charges `actionCost × ACTION_VALUE_CC` with
  `ACTION_VALUE_CC = 60` cc — the design's price of one action.
  `features.ts:421` divides by 100 and `w = −60` multiplies back, so an action
  spent relocating is scored at **36 cc, not 60 cc**. This is the one feature
  whose underlying quantity is already a cc PRICE rather than a count of
  material, so its weight is not a free parameter the way the others are: 100
  is the value that preserves `ACTION_VALUE_CC`.
- **KillAvailable's unit is crystals per action, not crystals.**
  `Σ (valueCc / minActions)` (`features.ts:360-370`) mixes a value with a rate;
  `div100` then calls the result crystals. The weight 35 reads as "cc per
  crystal-of-target per action", which is a different currency from every other
  `div100` feature's "cc per crystal".
- **`Infiltration` (11) is identically zero.** `geometry.ts:120-137` counts
  ordered pairs `(my unit at s, enemy unit at a)` with `s ∈ RECT[enemy][a]`.
  Because the corners are `(0,0)` and `(9,9)` and `RECT` is the axis-aligned
  box from a side's corner to the anchor (`tables.ts:116-117`),
  `s ∈ RECT[BLACK][a] ⟺ s.x ≥ a.x ∧ s.y ≥ a.y ⟺ a ∈ RECT[WHITE][s]`. The
  relation is symmetric, so the two sides' counts are equal pair for pair and
  the symmetric difference is 0 for every legal position. Measured:
  `infiltrationAnchors > 0` in 1,064/2,000 side-positions (max 15), and the
  FEATURE was zero in **1,000/1,000** positions. A weight of +90 on a named
  strategic concept (DESIGN's rationale: "NK:24 Tanka into B2; CA W7") buys
  nothing.
- **Constant-0 weights that still cost time.** `Corridor` (20) and `TierClimb`
  (21) have `w = 0` by DESIGN's own decision ("Texel decides", "never reward
  climbing by default"), but both are computed on every stage-1 evaluation
  (`features.ts:297-298`), each a full scan of the slot array, `tierClimb`
  additionally clearing a 6-entry scratch. They were non-zero in 280/1,000 and
  473/1,000 positions, contributing 0 cc every time.
- **Always-0 invariants.** Inv15 (`invariants.ts:345-348`) and Inv18
  (`invariants.ts:356-358`) are never set, and their weights are 0. DESIGN
  §5.13 says so for both (an em dash, and "not a feature (bit always 0)"), so
  this is documented intent, not drift — but `boundStage2` still adds
  `|w[i]|` for all twenty (`features.ts:568`), which is 0 for these two, so no
  bound is wasted.
- **`SpawnArea` is a raw square count at 30 cc/square.** Mean |contribution|
  **569 cc**, maximum **2,310 cc** on the corpus — larger than the catalogue
  prior of the dearest body in the game (plant_3/metal_3, 1,700 cc). It was the
  largest single term in 5 of the 16 exam loss roots
  (e.g. `loss-g4-s2_3_1-B-white-t2`, black, turn 2: SpawnArea −1,920 cc of a
  −3,164 cc score). `STRATEGIC_UNDERSTANDING.md` §6.4 lists "raw square count"
  as the DEFECT of the shipped evaluator's anchor term and asks instead for
  "anchor area × survivability, blocking-set size"; SU §7.1 phrases the target
  as a threshold ("≥ 1, prefer ≥ 3"), not a linear count.
- **`Inv3RetreatSquare` drops a conjunct DESIGN states.** DESIGN §5.13 row 3:
  "some own unit with `material ≥ 400` has `approach == RETREAT` **and the
  attacker has `retreats > 0`**". `invariants.ts:281-283` tests only
  `cls === Approach.RETREAT && cat.cost[def] >= 4`. `t.retreats[slot]` — "retreat
  squares of that attacker outside our strike" (`context.ts:103`) — is computed
  and sitting in the same table. The class is set whenever ANY free square is
  adjacent after the attack (`approach.ts:347-358`), while `retreats` counts
  only squares outside our strike, so `RETREAT ∧ retreats == 0` is exactly the
  case SU §7.3 excludes ("unless the attacker is itself killable afterwards").
  Measured: Inv3 fired in 965/2,000 side-positions, and in **943 of them every
  triggering unit had `retreats == 0`** — the penalty is on the wrong side of
  its own gate 97.7% of the time. Sample positions: `fuzz-5150-1651-27` (both
  sides, turn 5), `fuzz-5150-531-27` (black, turn 6), `fuzz-5150-1044-37`
  (black, turn 7).
- **A stale doc claim about `Material`.** `features.ts:28-31` says
  `Evaluator.stage0` "overwrites `out[F.Material]` with the weighted value in
  `full()`". It does not: `full()` copies `this.f` unchanged
  (`evaluate.ts:165-169`) and `extract` wrote the catalogue-prior version
  (`features.ts:174`). With `DEFAULT_WEIGHTS` the two agree exactly (every
  `materialCc` is a multiple of 100 and `w[Material] === 100`), so `Σ w·f`
  reproduces the score today; under a tuned `material` vector it will not.

## (c) Side symmetry

Two different questions, answered separately.

### Is the evaluation antisymmetric under swapping the root side?

Yes, measured. `extract(p, side=0) === −extract(p, side=1)` held for every one
of the 58 features on every one of the 1,000 corpus positions — **zero
violations**, including the 487 positions in the action phase and the 613 at
handicap 3. Repeating the sweep with `p.phase` forced to 1 and `p.actions`
forced to 2 (a synthetic mid-turn node) also gave zero violations on all 58.

This is by construction: every feature is written as `g(me) − g(them)` with one
code path (`features.ts:174-178, 278-299, 415-445`), the two features that read
side-relative state negate explicitly (`ActionsLeft` at `features.ts:296`,
`DrawPressure` through `leadCc` at `features.ts:293`), and `div100` truncates
towards zero so the rounding negates too.

### Do the two sides get the same QUESTION asked of them?

No. Four places treat the two sides differently at the same node.

- **Turn flags.** `resetUnitActions` (`src/game/board.ts:271-298`) clears
  `lastAttackKilled`, `placedThisTurn`, `promotedThisPlacement` and
  `damageTaken` on the INCOMING player at the turn boundary. On a macro node
  after side S's turn, only S still carries them. Invariants 5, 7, 8, 9, 14, 17
  and 20 read exactly those flags, so at a macro node they can only be violated
  by the side that just moved — except invariant 14, which INVERTS: the waiting
  side's `killedThisTurn` is false by construction, so it takes the −200 on any
  `bank < 6`. Measured (just-moved / to-move): inv5 52/14, inv8 114/27,
  inv9 21/4, inv17 12/5, inv20 78/18, inv14 821/921. The score therefore shifts
  with ply parity, which is a systematic offset between sibling levels of the
  search rather than an evaluation error at any one node.
- **`ActionsLeft` at a phase-1 node.** `features.ts:296` returns `±p.actions`
  whenever `p.phase === 1`, with no test on whether any action has been spent.
  DESIGN §5.12.1 row 19 says "0 at macro nodes", which holds inside the search
  only because a completed turn lands in the place phase (`state.ts:495`,
  `setPhase(p, 1)` at `END_PLACE`). 487 of the 1,000 corpus positions are in
  the action phase with all four actions left, and score **±160 cc** for the
  side to move.
- **Kill-table budgets.** `buildLevel2` (`context.ts:236-254`) builds
  `killNow[mover]` with the mover's REMAINING actions and `killNow[other]` with
  a full 4. `KillAvailable` (33), `StrandPunish` (32), invariant 4 and
  invariant 16 all read `killNow`, so mid-turn the mover is asked "what can you
  still do this turn" while the opponent is asked "what could you do in a whole
  turn". At a macro node the budgets coincide (both 4).
- **Handicap crystals.** `blackCrystalHandicap` reaches the score only through
  the bank (`BankLiquid` + `BankConvertible` = 110 cc/crystal, plus
  `BankExcess` above 8) and through `leadCc` (DrawPressure, invariant 16). At
  the corpus's `handicap: 3` that is a standing +330 cc for Black before a move
  is made. `Material` never sees it (`state.ts:1533-1549` counts bodies only).

`upkeepPending` is a fourth side-relative flag but no feature reads it; only
`invariants.ts`'s module header mentions it, as the one node type where an
enemy's damage is still visible to invariant 9.

## (d) Material

- `p.materialCc[side]` is `Σ cost[def] × 100` over that side's LIVING units:
  `rehash` recomputes exactly that (`state.ts:1533-1549`), `linkSquare`/
  `unlinkSquare` maintain it incrementally (`state.ts:359,363`).
  `sq[slot] === DEAD` is skipped everywhere, and `allocState` fills `sq` with
  `DEAD` (`state.ts:127`), so dead and unused slots contribute nothing.
- A promoted unit is counted at the PROMOTED definition's catalogue cost, once.
  `makePromote` adds `promoCost × CC` (`state.ts:1070`) and
  `getPromotionCost` is `next.cost − current.cost` (`src/game/units.ts:278-283`),
  so `cost[old] + promoCost === cost[new]`. The incremental sum and `rehash`'s
  recomputation agree, which is why `check()` never trips on it. The tier-1
  body's purchase price is therefore NOT double-credited: it is absorbed into
  the promoted body's cost, which is what the player actually paid in total.
- The same promotion is then charged and credited elsewhere, all legitimately
  distinct quantities but all at once:
  - `Rent` −422 cc for the +1 upkeep every tier-2 body carries (tier-1 upkeep
    is 0 for all six elements; tier-2 is 1; tier-3 is 2).
  - `EconDelta` a further ≈ −305 cc for the same upkeep (§(a)).
  - `BankLiquid` + `BankConvertible` −110 cc per crystal of promotion cost.
  - `PstMine` and `EconDelta`'s income leg for any change in mine rate
    (fire_1 → fire_2 changes none: both mine 1).
  - `Inv7PromoteNoRunway` −600 cc if `turnsToInsolvency ≤ 6`.
  - Worked example, fire_1 → fire_2 at promotion cost 4 with the bank at or
    below 8: material +400, bank −440, Rent −422, EconDelta's rent leg −305 =
    **−767 cc before any tactical term**, and −1,367 with Inv7.
- `Evaluator.materialCc` (`evaluate.ts:103-112`) scores the material block from
  the tunable 18-parameter `w.material`, NOT from `p.materialCc`. They agree
  exactly under `DEFAULT_WEIGHTS`, where `material[d] === cost[d] × 100`
  (`config.ts:238-246`, checked by `materialIsCataloguePrior`,
  `weights.ts:148-152`). Under a tuned vector the promoted unit is valued at
  `material[promoted def]`, while the reported feature 0 keeps the catalogue
  prior — see §(b)'s last bullet.
- Handicap crystals are not material (above).

## Findings, ranked by expected cost to play

Ranked by how much cc the defect moves on a typical position × how often it
fires, not by how surprising it is.

1. **Rent is charged twice** (§(a)): −727 cc per crystal of upkeep instead of
   −422. Every tier-2/tier-3 body and every promotion is mispriced by 72%
   against the design's own `RENT_PV`. Judge: canonical fact
   (`income.ts:94-102` and `economy.ts:175` compute the same sum) plus DESIGN
   §5.12's explicit "charged once".
2. **A vulnerable body is charged on up to five features at once** (§(a)):
   mean 313 cc against a mean prior of 391 cc, 130% in the worst measured case.
   The engine pays nearly a whole unit to avoid a threat the opponent has not
   spent an action on.
3. **`Inv3RetreatSquare` fires on the case its own definition excludes**
   (§(b)): −250 cc in 943 of its 965 firings. Judge: authored judgment
   (DESIGN §5.13 row 3, SU §7.3) plus canonical fact (`t.retreats` exists).
4. **`blocking ≤ 1` is priced three times** (§(a)): up to −660 cc, on 55% of
   side-positions.
5. **`DepletionWaste` is the economy DP counted a second time, undiscounted**
   (§(b)): identity exact in 83% of side-positions, up to 1,650 cc.
6. **`SpawnArea` is a raw square count worth more than the best unit in the
   game** (§(b)): up to 2,310 cc, and the largest term in 5 of the 16 exam loss
   roots. Judge: authored judgment (SU §6.4 names raw square count as the
   defect; SU §7.1 wants a threshold).
7. **`Inv4StrandUnpunished`'s punisher test is vacuous** (§(a)): inert in 97%
   of its 1,073 firings, so it duplicates `ApproachStrand` at a flat −100.
8. **`EconDelta` over-subtracts `PstMine`'s baseline** (§(b)): the net weight
   on standing mining PV is −20 cc per crystal.
9. **`SpawnZero`/`Inv1` are one condition at −1,600 cc**, and `HomeInvaded`/
   `Inv10` are one condition at −4,400 cc, and `RunwayCliff`/`Insolvency` are
   one condition at −1,350 cc (§(a)). Rare but decisive when they fire.
10. **`Infiltration` is identically zero** (§(b)): a weighted concept that can
    never contribute.
11. **Six invariants only accuse the side that just moved** (§(c)), and
    `Inv14` inverts, so the eval carries a ply-parity offset.
12. **`RelocationDebt` prices an action at 36 cc where the design says 60**
    (§(b)).
13. **`Corridor`, `TierClimb`, `Inv15`, `Inv18` cost time and contribute
    nothing** (§(b)).

## Caveats

- The corpus is the M5 differential fuzzer's sample, not game play. It is the
  right corpus for "does this feature ever fire and is it antisymmetric", and
  the wrong one for "how much does this cost in a real game". Frequencies here
  are not frequencies in the champion's search tree.
- Half the corpus (487/1,000) sits in the action phase with four actions left,
  which is not what a macro node in the search looks like. Everything except
  the `ActionsLeft` measurement is unaffected (the rest of stage 1 and all of
  stage 2 do not read `p.phase`).
- The mid-turn antisymmetry sweep forced `p.phase`/`p.actions` directly and did
  not update `kturn`; tables were invalidated by hand each time. It shows
  `extract` is antisymmetric at such a node; it does not prove such nodes are
  reachable with those exact field values.
- Everything here is static evaluation. A double count that the search can see
  through costs less than the raw cc suggests; a wrong SIGN cannot be searched
  away. The rank ordering above assumes the first but not the second.
- No claim here is based on the adviser or on the champion at more work, per
  the E3 judge rule. The judges used are canonical fact (code that computes a
  quantity twice; predicates that are literally identical), authored judgment
  (DESIGN §5.12–§5.13 and SU §6.4/§7 as statements of the strategic model), and
  measurement over a named corpus.
- The E3.1 brief says the 16 exam loss cases "each carry a full canonical
  state". They do not: 16/16 carry `position.kind === "recipe"` (an opening id
  plus an action list) and are replayed by `exam/format.ts loadCaseState`. That
  is what this audit used.
- Contribution magnitudes, group sums and stage costs belong to lane 4's
  instrument; the per-feature profile above is a by-product of this audit's own
  sweep and is not the calibrated version.
- Invariant semantics in depth (healing, quiet clock, UNKNOWN verdicts) belong
  to lane 2; the invariant claims here are limited to where an invariant
  duplicates or contradicts a feature.
