# Muju Academy — corrected curriculum and production batches
Owner decisions applied 10 September 2026 · 27 scripts · rules v2.2

Use `final/R01.md` through `R27.md`, or `ALL-FINAL-SCRIPTS.md`. These R-numbers belong to the revised course; they do not map one-to-one onto the fourteen older published videos. The table describes what the scripts actually teach, not a promise that a short course exhausts high-level play.

## The learning sequence

R01–R06 establish the board, movement, combat and economy. R07–R10 teach the remaining turn rules and play a complete example. R11–R16 revisit those ideas through the six elements. R17–R26 develop tactics and strategic judgment. R27 combines the habits in a three-station finale.

Upkeep is first taught explicitly in R07, draws in R09. Keep both topics out of R01/R02. The opening's general "if the game is still going" condition remains so the home rule is accurate.

Production order follows viewing order to catch continuity early. Batches are units of production and review, not a recommendation that a child must watch three or four videos in one sitting. R10 and R27 already have natural stopping points inside an episode.

## Before committing to the full render run

1. Audition the existing voices with the proposed delivery instructions and name/coordinate aliases. Listen to the proposed thinking tones; no need to recast familiar characters.
2. Finish R02 as a full pilot. Use short R04 and R11 samples to exercise the income reveal and guest dialogue before mass rendering.
3. Let an actual child watch if convenient. Look for an attempt at the question and a usable idea afterward; distinguish observed feedback from agent role-play. This is a useful check, not a claim that one viewer establishes a universal teaching method.
4. Finish one batch at a time. Recheck the changed board examples, listen to the dialogue, and inspect actual video frames. Measure duration after speech synthesis; no runtime or cost estimate is established by this script pass.
5. Version replacement videos and pages with the actual update time. Map revised R-IDs to release IDs deliberately so a child is not silently shown a different lesson under the old title.

The advanced section teaches applied calculation, preparation, economic forecasting, denial, priorities and uncertainty about an opponent's choice. Expert-level opening theory, long forcing combinations and comparative full-game analysis would need further worked games and puzzles; the current scripts do not prove a best opening or an optimal strategy.
